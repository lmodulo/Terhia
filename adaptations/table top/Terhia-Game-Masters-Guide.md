# Terhia — Game Master's Guide

Tabletop rules, draft v0.3 (24 September 2026). Companion to the Player's Manual (PM); page references below are to PM sections. Built against Terhia-Canon v1.1. v0.3 adds relics as loot (§9), the relic hooks in §3, §4, §6, §7, §8, and §10, and register rows 27–39.

**Scope.** Adjudication procedures, the layer sheet, records, the full Line rules, opposition blocks for practitioners and ordinary people, downtime and advancement, relics as loot, templates, and a register of design decisions that touch canon. Not covered here: grand beasts, factions, institutions, cultures, money *(modules)*. Where a module would supply an input, the rule below names the input and gives an interim rule; the interim rules draw on the Canon sections in the table.

**Reading order.** Before the first session: PM sections 1–8 and sections 1–5 of this guide. Sections 6–8 when the first engagement, Line, or downtime phase arrives; section 9 when the first relic is found. Of the Canon, Part XI (working reference) and the Part II–IV cost sections are the routine load; the full file only when a session touches canon.

## 1. The GM's three duties

The rules put three jobs on the GM that a generic game does not.

1. **Price everything.** Every technique has a cost, the cost is paid in the body, and the body keeps the account. The GM's job is to make the price visible: name the phase, state the Strain before the roll, and narrate what a stage looks like from the outside.
2. **Partition the picture.** Every scene is prepared in layers (section 4). A reading returns one layer at one grade, to one player, privately. The GM never narrates the joined picture. The players build it or do not.
3. **Keep records, not facts.** Every number the players receive is a record with a source (section 5). The GM does not say how many; the GM says who counted, and what they counted.

Everything else in this guide is procedure for those three.

## 2. Setting Difficulty

Use the PM ladder (10 / 13 / 16 / 19 / 22 / 26 / 30). Guidance for placing a task on it:

| Difficulty | Set it when |
|:--|:--|
| 10 Easy | A trained person nearly always manages; roll only if failure is interesting |
| 13 Moderate | The default for a task with a real chance of failure |
| 16 Hard | Needs skill or luck; a Novice fails more often than not |
| 19 Severe | Adept-level work at the edge; a Master expects to manage |
| 22 Master-grade | Only a Master's Rank Die makes it likely |
| 26 Guru-grade | A Guru manages roughly a third of the time; a Master only on a strong roll |
| 30 Beyond one hand | Not set for a single check. It is a **Join** (section 4) |

**Opposed checks.** Both roll; higher total wins; ties go to the defender or to whoever is maintaining the status quo. Each side adds their Rank Die only for school work.

**When not to roll.** A Master doing Novice work of their own school does not roll unless opposed; describe the result and charge the Strain. A Guru's Rank Die makes Easy and Moderate school tasks automatic; the price is still paid.

**Favor and Hindrance** stack from circumstance: prepared ground, a reading in hand, a tool built for the job, an advantage of numbers. Two sources of Favor are still one Favor.

## 3. Running the ledger

### Strain and overdraw at the table

- State a technique's cost before the player commits, with the phase modifier already in it ("four this phase, not three"). The modifier is written on the sheet at the phase turn (PM §6, Procedure); nobody opens the moon table mid-scene.
- Overdraw is the player's decision. When a technique would exceed Capacity, say the number of Marks it will cost and let them choose. Never overdraw a player character on their behalf.
- Marks come from overdraw, from Mark-costed techniques, and from a few standing situations: a Guru's *Sustained Work* per phase, a *Borrow* held past its scene, a *Hold the Door* per turn, a Gate open past its span, a potion's bill, a Guru relic's tether per phase (PM §10). Environmental Strain (a Novice reader on a battlefield; a Guru inside their own dilated volume) is charged at the start of the affected character's turn. A relic's feed (PM §10) is charged when the relic is used or, for a per-scene feed, at the start of the wearer's first turn in the scene; state it as any cost is stated.
- A Held technique's upkeep is due at the start of the maintaining character's turn. If they cannot or will not pay, the hold drops before they act. If damage broke their attention out of turn, the hold dropped then.

### Narrating stage

A stage is written on the player's sheet as mechanics. It is written on the character's body as something other people see. Use both:

- On reaching a stage, ask the player to describe it once, in their own words, as a physical fact — the tremor, the pallor, the shoulder that sits high. Record the sentence.
- Thereafter, do not remind the player. Let other characters notice: a stranger who reads the pallor; a child who waits on the stairs. Stage penalties are applied by the rules; the recognition is narrated by the world.
- A stage never reverses. If a scene seems to call for it — a great healing, a god's favor — the answer is *Years for Years*: Marks reset at Stage 4, the stage itself untouched.

### The clock

Marks are the character's clock, and the rules do not hide it. Between creation and the End, a Novice has fifty Marks, an Adept forty, a Master thirty, a Guru twenty. Say the number to the player at creation, and again at each rank.

The rate is the table's. Marks come only from overdraw, from Mark-costed techniques, and from the standing charges listed above; a Guru who casts within Capacity and holds no standing work takes none. At one or two Marks a session — one overdraw, or one *Transfer* — a Guru is played for ten to twenty sessions; at none, indefinitely. Two tunables are already registered (§11, rows 4 and 15): fifteen Marks per stage for a slower burn, and the standing charge on *Sustained Work*. The *Unread Ledger* changes who sees the clock, not its rate.

Stage 3 and Stage 4 are the Guru's body as the rules price it, not a fault in the rules. A Stage 3 character is played as one whose remainder is known, by the player and, in time, by the world. A table that wants the same characters for years plays Adepts and Masters who decline to sit (§8, *Declining*); a table that wants Gurus plays them at their price. Decide which at the start of the campaign, aloud.

### The End

When a Stage 4 practitioner reaches 10 Marks, stop the engagement if one is running (the character finishes the turn on which it happened, and the End takes the rest of the scene). Then run the End as a scene with the player: where the character is, who is present, what the school's End looks like from inside and from outside. The class entry names the outcome; the scene gives it a place. The character leaves play at the end of the scene. Death and departure are both Ends; the rules do not rank them.

If the table prefers an End to land at a chosen moment rather than mid-fight, allow the player to *carry* the tenth Mark: the End is deferred to the end of the session, during which the character may act at Stage 4 with no further Mark tally kept; any Mark the character would take in that time is paid and discarded, since the End is already fixed. This is a table option; the default is that the End begins the moment the tenth Mark lands.

### Option: the Unread Ledger

A table that wants cost hidden from the practitioner as well as from others may have the GM keep Marks. The player tracks Strain and knows when they overdrew; the GM records the Marks and announces a stage only when it shows. Use this only with a table that has agreed to it, and never for the Mark-costed Guru techniques, whose cost the practitioner chooses.

## 4. The layer sheet

### Preparing a scene

For any scene where a reading may be used — which is most of them — prepare a **layer sheet**: one line per layer, holding what that layer would return, plus the **join**, which is the GM's own account of what is true and is never read aloud.

Not every layer has content in every scene. A layer with nothing in it returns "nothing here" honestly, and that is information. Prepare the layers that matter; for the rest, be ready to say "nothing" without hesitation.

### What a reading returns, by layer and grade

| Layer | Novice (one fact, vague) | Adept (two facts, one precise) | Master (three, precise; Circle) | Guru (entire; Ground) |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Structure** | Rough weight or motion of one thing touched or watched | Precise mass, speed, and composition of what is touched or watched a full turn | Every object in the Circle as data; where each will be next turn | The Ground as data; and the two values that accept editing |
| **Life** | Whether nearby flora and fauna are well, sick, or dying | Where the damage sits on a touched body; what it needs | Every living thing in the Circle, through walls, with its condition | The Ground alive: every animal, the state of a forest, a river's health |
| **Boundary** | Someone died here; roughly when; roughly how many | The register and the time of a crossing; the cause in general | The moment of crossing in detail, as the dead experienced it; the killer as seen; deaths told apart across centuries | Every death on the Ground; the battlefield's whole account |
| **Intent** | Where fear, calm, or hostility sits among those in view | What one person wants or means this moment | Who in the Circle will break, who will hold, who is lying | The intent of a crowd or a Line entire |
| **Element** | What the element near you is doing | Which element answers here; what it does next turn | All four elements in the Circle and their next moves | Weather, water table, fire lines, the ground's stability across the Ground |
| **Make** | Whether a thing is bench-made or dosed | What a flask holds; a gadget's function; the maker's school and rank; a dose's onset | Traits of the maker; whom a dose was tailored to; the layout of a system | The whole pharmacopoeia or household in play |
| **Field — Weight** (Sword) | Which foe commits next | To what, and where the opening is | Every commitment in the Circle before it is made | A formation's options, retiring one by one |
| **Field — Geometry** (Glaive) | The nearest corridor or gap | Where the melee flows next | The hinge, the corridor, and where the melee will be next turn across the Circle | The whole field as space the Guru can deny |
| **Field — Ground** (Longbow) | Distance, cover, the safe path | Where a moving target will be; the trail's age | Every approach to a held position | The landscape as ground a formation will or will not cross |
| **Field — Seams** (Hammer) | Where the nearest thing breaks | The join in armor or a barricade | A gate's load path; a charge's stopping point | Where a formation's weight is stored |
| **Field — Nerve** (Ax) | Whether one creature holds or breaks | Whether the line holds or breaks this turn | — (the school narrows at Master: no Master reading) | — |
| **Plain** (anyone, by Notice) | What eyes, ears, and hands give. Reckoning returns it measured; Attunement returns it felt |

**Field readings outside an engagement.** The Field layer is read wherever bodies and structures are, not only in a fight, and the layer sheet for an investigative scene carries a Field line as an engagement's does. *Weight:* in any room, who is about to act, and whether a threat is posture. *Geometry:* how a building, a street, or a market channels movement — where a crowd will go; which door a place forces you through. *Ground:* the safe route across country; whether ground has been crossed, and a trail's age (*Track*, at Adept, makes it a check). *Seams:* where a bridge, a hull, a wall, or a gate will fail, and what one blow would open. *Nerve:* whether a person under pressure — questioned, bargaining, threatened — holds or breaks. Grades apply as in the table: a Novice reads one thing near at hand; a Master, the Circle. The Field says nothing about make, life, structure, intent, or the dead; a Field reader in a poisoning returns "nothing here."

**Reading a relic.** A relic holds information in layers as a beast does (Grand Beasts §1), and the partition is the point: no layer returns a relic's effect, its governor, and its provenance together.

| Layer | Returns about a relic |
|:--|:--|
| **Plain** (Notice) | An object; the Trait if it is visible; a Rigidity at Difficulty 13 after a scene of wearing |
| **Structure** | Mass; at Adept, a reading where the map has none — its ground's signature; at Guru, a tether's two editable values |
| **Life** | What it needs: the Dependency |
| **Boundary** | What it killed, when, in what register; whose grave it came from |
| **Intent** | Nothing here |
| **Element** | The cell it expresses; what it does at the next phase turn — the Inversion |
| **Make** | Seated or not; the seater's school and rank; at Master, whom the seating was tailored to. Nothing about the relic's source, which was not made |
| **Field — Seams** | Where the piece breaks |
| **Field — Weight, Geometry, Ground, Nerve** | Nothing here |

Two constraints the table holds:

- **A reading is true and partial.** Never return a false fact from a reading. Return a fact that is true from inside the layer and misleading outside it: the Boundary reading gives the killer as the dead saw them, which is not the killer.
- **A layer does not know what it cannot see.** The Structure layer returns "nothing here" for a grief; the Intent layer returns "nothing here" for a load path. Say "nothing" cleanly. Do not hint at other layers.

### Delivery

Readings are delivered **to the reading player alone** — a passed note, a whisper, a side message, a step into the next room. The other players hear what the character says. This is not optional in the default rules; it is the mechanism by which the design principle happens at the table. A table that plays with open readings should know it is playing a different game and decide so once, out loud.

The fastest delivery is the layer sheet itself, prepared as one card per layer: hand the card, take it back. Writing a note during play is the slow form; keep it for readings the sheet did not anticipate.

**Relay.** What the reading player says aloud is their sentence, not the GM's fact. Apply the Tongue rule (PM §9) when the relay crosses a language line. Do not correct a relay that has drifted. If a player asks the GM "is that what my reading said," answer only the reading player, privately.

### The Join

Any Difficulty 30 task requires two practitioners of different schools each to succeed at Difficulty 22 on their own layer of the same problem, in the same scene. Both pay their costs. The result is one outcome no single school reaches.

Standing Joins — outcomes that exist only this way:

| Join | Schools | Outcome |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Return** | Necrotic Guru *Hold the Door* + Divine Guru *Transfer* on the held creature before the hold ends | The crossing does not complete. The creature returns Downed at 1 Vigor. The Necromancer has paid a Mark per turn of the hold; the Apostle pays *Transfer*'s 2 M. No other combination of rules brings a crossed creature back |
| **Unmask a contact poison** | Dark Arts Master *Contact-Grade* antidote alone; or Light Arts Master *Bench Reading* (the dose named) + Nature Master *Channel Work* (three turns) | The poison ends. The Light-plus-Nature route is the one available to anyone who cannot buy a Warlock |
| **Locate the unseen** | Arcane *Overlay* (a shape where nothing should be) + Nature *Ward-Read* (a living thing there) or Necrotic *Attune* (a crossing there) | A *Never Seen* Warden, a hidden practitioner, or a familiar is placed. Either layer alone returns an anomaly, not a position |
| **Certify** | *(institutions module)* | A reading made admissible: which schools and which witness, per the certifying body. Until the module exists: the Cobalt Ledger certifies in Accord territory, where a Boundary reading is never admissible; the Indigo Assay in Crown territory; no body in Chimera territory. The GM names the second school the body requires (Canon Part II, Necrotic institutional role; Part VI, Institution Summary) |
| **Master-grade task at Guru difficulty** | Any two schools whose layers both bear on the task | The general rule: the GM names the two layers that would have to agree |

When players propose a Join the guide does not list, the test is: do two layers each hold a necessary part, and does neither hold the whole? If so, allow it at 22 + 22.

## 5. Records

**The counter rule.** A number given to the players comes from someone who counted, and the GM names the counter with the number: "the toll-keeper's tally says forty came through," not "forty came through." The players may always ask who counted, and the answer is always available.

**Two records.** For any figure that matters, prepare two counts from two counters that disagree, and hand out whichever the scene puts in reach. Both are true as records. Do not adjudicate between them unless the players find the counting error — and when they do, that is a discovery, not a retcon.

**Readings are records too.** A Boundary reading is the dead's count. A Structure reading is the instrument's. The layer sheet's join is the only account the GM holds as fact, and it stays unread.

**Rules of thumb for what to give.** Orders of magnitude, freely. Exact figures, only as records. Averages, only with the population that was averaged. Rates, only with the phase they were taken in.

## 6. Engagements

### Initiative, surprise, and readiness

Per PM §7. Additional rulings:

- A character on *Watch* or *Overwatch* may act **before** the first round: one Ready or one attack against the first thing to move.
- A practitioner maintaining a Held technique when an engagement begins pays its upkeep at the start of their first turn as usual.
- A *Read the Chant* Reaction resolves before the technique it interrupts; if the Hexblade misses, the technique proceeds.

### Scale

Four scale words govern what a technique reaches: **Personal** (one target or self), **Circle** (10 paces around the practitioner, 20 for Master readings), **Line** (a formation, treated as one entity), **Ground** (a landscape feature; a valley, a harbor, a ward; 100 paces and beyond). Ranks gate scale: Novice reaches Personal; Adept Personal and small Circles; Master the Circle and the Line; Guru the Line and the Ground. Do not let a lower rank reach a larger scale by clever phrasing; the scale word in the technique is the limit.

### Lines

A Line is a formation run as one entity. It has **Cohesion** instead of Vigor.

| Line | Foot | Cohesion | Guard | Attack | Damage | Move |
|:--|:-:|:-:|:-:|:--|:--|:-:|
| Squad | 10 | 6 | 12 | d20 + Cohesion ÷ 2 | d10 + Cohesion ÷ 4 | 4 |
| Platoon | 40 | 12 | 12 | d20 + Cohesion ÷ 2 | d10 + Cohesion ÷ 4 | 4 |
| Column | 160 | 20 | 12 | d20 + Cohesion ÷ 2 | d10 + Cohesion ÷ 4 | 3 |

Divisions round down. Shields: Guard +1. An Adept officer: Guard +1 and Cohesion checks +2. A Master officer: Guard +2, Cohesion checks +4.

- **Attacks.** A Line attacks once per turn against every enemy adjacent to it. Its reach is the reach of its members' weapons.
- **Cohesion loss.** Techniques that name Cohesion deal it directly. All other damage dealt to members goes into a pool the GM keeps; every 10 points in the pool is 1 Cohesion. A Line's officer Downed: −d6. A Line that sees another Line break: Cohesion check at 13 or −d4.
- **Cohesion checks.** d20 + Cohesion ÷ 2 (+ officer bonus) against the Difficulty a technique names, or 13 to advance under fire, 16 to hold against a Guru.
- **Line against Line.** Each turn both make opposed Cohesion checks; the loser takes d6 Cohesion; a tie costs both d4.
- **Breaking.** At 0 Cohesion the Line breaks. Run up to five members as individuals (use the ordinary-person or Novice block below); the rest flee, rout, or surrender as the scene allows. A broken Line cannot reform until a full rest, and reforms at half Cohesion.
- **Rally.** An officer or any character with Standing may spend an Act on a Standing check at 16 to restore d6 Cohesion, once per engagement per Line.

**The platoon standard.** A Guru of any martial school is built to engage a Platoon alone, and the Guru techniques are calibrated to break a Cohesion of 12 in two to four turns while paying 5 + 3 per turn. A Master engages a Squad on similar terms. An Adept fights two or three foot at once; a Novice, one. Use these when judging an encounter: a Line of a given size against the party's ranks.

### Held effects and interruptions

- Damage to a maintaining practitioner: Reserve check at 10 + damage (12 + damage for a Guru edit). Roll it at once, before continuing.
- Two Held techniques from one practitioner: allowed if both are paid and both have their action each turn. An Act and a Ready in one turn can maintain one of each.
- When a hold drops, its effect ends at that instant. A slowed region resumes; a braced beam falls; a Circle stops attacking; a Boon lifts.

### The Gate at the table

The Berserker's *Open the Gate* forces the nearest hostile as the target; the GM decides "nearest" by paces and breaks ties toward the last creature that struck the Berserker. Under *All the Way*, the turn after nothing hostile is in reach, the Berserker attacks the nearest creature of any kind. Announce the count of open turns aloud every round; the *Calling-Back* Difficulty rises with it, and the table should see the number climbing.

### Dilate and Weigh

Cost worked examples, for reading the tables quickly:

| Edit | Ratio factor | Radius factor | Cast | Upkeep |
|:--|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Slow a 5-pace region to half | 1 | 2 | 10 | 3 |
| Slow a 1-pace region to a quarter | 2 | 1 | 10 | 6 |
| Double a 20-pace region | 1 | 3 | 15 | 3 |
| Pin a 5-pace region | — | 2 | 10 | 3 |

Add the Overlay's own upkeep (1 per turn) to every line. A Guru at Capacity 20 casts one of these and holds it two or three turns before overdraw. That is the intended rate.

### Healing economy

Vigor restores at full rest only, at Reserve modifier + 4. Nature Masters restore it turn by turn at Strain; Divine Masters restore it at once for Marks; Divine Adepts lend it until rest. Poisons and diseases are ended by purgatives at grade, by three turns of *Channel Work*, or by *Transfer*. A Vigor relic (PM §10) converts Strain to Vigor and is priced on the ledger, as *Redirect* is. Nothing else heals. Do not introduce potions of healing into the Dark Arts: *Past the Budget* is the potion, and its bill is the point; a relic that healed for free would be the same mistake in an object.

## 7. Opposition

### Practitioner blocks

Use these for any practitioner who is not a player character. Attribute modifiers are assumed +1 / +2 / +3 / +4 by rank for the keyed attribute and +1 for Reserve at all ranks; adjust if a named opponent needs it.

| Rank | Rank Die | Guard (martial / other) | Vigor (martial / alchemy / magic) | Strain Capacity | Attack (school weapon) | Stage |
|:--|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:--|:-:|
| Novice | d4 | 13 / 12 | 13 / 11 / 9 | 10 | +1 + d4 | 0 |
| Adept | d6 | 15 / 12 | 17 / 15 / 13 | 12 | +2 + d6 | 1 |
| Master | d8 | 17 / 12 | 21 / 19 / 17 | 14 | +3 + d8 | 2 |
| Guru | d12 | 19 / 12 | 25 / 23 / 21 | 18 | +4 + d12 | 3 |

Add armor to Guard. Give each block the school's techniques at its rank; do not run a non-player Master with fewer than two techniques of each rank at or below theirs. Non-player practitioners overdraw at the GM's discretion and pay the same Marks, which is how a recurring opponent ages across a campaign. A non-player practitioner may carry seated relics (PM §10): apply the Capacity cost to the block, write the relic on the cost file, and turn a Master relic at the phase turn as a player's turns.

### Ordinary people

The majority of anyone the party meets: Guard 10 (+armor), Vigor 6, attack +0, damage by weapon, no Rank Die, no Strain. A latent has a Knack (PM §11). An officer of a Line is an Adept or Master of a martial school.

### How many

The number of practitioners at each rank a place can field is a record — the Ledger's, the Assay's, or nobody's — and Canon Part II, Rank Distribution, holds the published figures: on the Ledger's roll, 310 Novices, 85 Adepts, and 14 Masters per 10,000 registered, and Gurus as a roll of names rather than a rate. Hand them out as records: the Assay's count for Crown territory is lower, and the Concord keeps none. In play: a settlement's Masters are few, its Gurus are named individuals, and a Novice is a common sight. An engagement with more than one Master on the other side is a major scene; one with a Guru is a campaign event.

### Encounter calibration

Rate the party by its ranks against the platoon standard above, then set the opposition at the scale the scene needs. The rules are not built for balanced fights; they are built for fights whose cost is legible. A party that wins by overdrawing has paid for the win, and the ledger carries it forward. Decide before the scene how much the opposition is willing to overdraw, and hold to it.

A party without a Nature or Divine Master heals at rest only, Reserve + 4 a night. Count the engagements between rests accordingly, and let the players count them too; the count is a record they can ask for.

## 8. Rest, downtime, and advancement

### Rest

Short rest: an hour, no exertion; Strain halved. Full rest: eight hours; Strain cleared; Vigor restored by Reserve + 4; one level of Exhausted cleared. Stage effects that restrict rest override this. Bench work happens in rest and is charged to the day's Strain; a Warden's *Watch* is compatible with rest for everyone but the Warden at Stage 2 and above.

### Phases and the calendar

Track the current phase on the table: **Velquor → Saelura → Orrivane → Namaris → Threnis**, 80 days each, 400-day year, the first day of the year the longest night, in Velquor. Announce the phase at the start of each session and at every phase turn. At each phase turn, every magic player writes the school's modifier for the new phase on their sheet, and the GM writes it on every non-player cost file (PM §6, Procedure). Pricing is then read off the sheet; the moon table is not opened mid-scene, and the rule for every priced scene is only that the cost is stated before the roll.

### Downtime accounting, per phase

- A Guru's *Sustained Work* standing: 1 Mark.
- A Nature Guru's standing *Borrow*: 1 Mark per scene it stands, charged as scenes are played.
- A Vanguard at Stage 2: drill each morning or the day's Guard bonus is halved.
- An Artificer's or Warlock's bonds: capacity stays reduced; a released bond restores it after a scene of unbinding.
- Bench stock: flasks keep for a phase from the day of making; potions keep for a phase; poisons keep for a year.
- Seating a relic (PM §10): an Artificer's bench work at the tier's Strain cost, or a Master-trade smith or jeweler for a day and a seal per tier (Novice and Adept relics only). Unseating: a scene, and the Capacity returns. A Master relic that enters its far phases stays seated at its Inversion until someone unseats it; reseating it needs a Master Artificer. A Guru relic's tether: 1 Mark per phase seated.
- Cutting a socket into a Novice piece: a Master-trade smith or jeweler, a day and a seal.

### Advancement adjudication

- **Techniques.** One per session; a second for a session in which the character's school work was decisive (the GM's judgment, stated at the end of the session). "Decisive" means a reading or technique without which the scene would have gone otherwise.
- **Boards.** Novice to Adept and Adept to Master require an examiner or teacher of the higher rank and a downtime phase *(institutions module for who sits the board)*. Until the module exists: a teacher exists if the story has put one in reach, and a board is sat before the body that keeps the territory's roll — the Cobalt Ledger, the Indigo Assay, or, in Chimera territory, no board at all (Canon Part VI, Institution Summary).
- **Guru.** A Guru teacher, or a Guru-grade deed witnessed and recorded. A deed is Guru-grade when it succeeded at Difficulty 26 or engaged a Line at the platoon standard; "witnessed and recorded" means a record exists — someone counted, and it is written or told.
- **Declining.** A character who does not sit keeps their rank and stage. Do not push. A campaign of careful Adepts is a campaign these rules support.

## 9. Relics

The player's rules — slots, tiers, sockets, the bond, the effect table — are PM §10. This section is the drop, the entry, the paper, and the rulings at the table.

### Where a relic comes from

Emergences, and nothing else (Grand Beasts §2). An Artificer seats a relic; no one makes one. The four outcome lines:

| Tier | A relic exists when | It is |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Novice | The Exploit was executed, or the beast was killed with its Rigidity known | The Trait's material: a coat, a tooth, a display, a footing |
| Adept | The Disruption was executed | Material from the Dependency site: a den-shard, a spring-stone, a lick's salt, roost-timber |
| Master | The beast was killed after its Reversal | The Dominance's remains |
| Guru | The Displacement Join succeeded | A fragment of the tether |

A Novice beast killed on its block with nothing known leaves a carcass. An Adept beast killed while its Dependency stands leaves an animal's body; the enhancement went back into the site, which is the relic's source and is still there. A Master beast driven off leaves nothing. Loot follows the reading, not the kill.

A relic found rather than taken — a grave-good, a thing in a vault, a piece in a market with a story attached — carries the same fields. Its ground and moon are what a record or the Boundary layer returns; where neither returns them, the GM writes them, and the relic's record says "unknown," as a record may.

### Writing the entry

Designation in the XI.3 form (ground, descriptor, phase and year of record) · tier · base animal · moon · domain · effect, one line from the PM §10 table · governor, one line from the tier's row · Capacity · record (who counted). The template is in §10.

The beast's own line names the domain: a speed trait is Movement; a bite or a horn is Damage; a coat or a carapace is Guard; a fire cell is Burning; a venom is Poison; a Saelura healing cell is Vigor; a den-site reading, or any reading the map lacks, is Reading; an enhancement that drew on the ground's depth is Strain. Where the beast's line fits no domain, choose the nearest; do not write a ninth. For a Novice relic, take the Rigidity from the beast's own Rigidity where it fits an object: a beast that hunted only by night leaves a trait that works only by night.

Frequency is the beast's. A party holds relics at the rate it ends emergences the right way — on the record, several Novice relics a phase for a working party, an Adept in a year, a Master in a career.

### The paper

A relic's body is the same on any ground; the paper is not (Grand Beasts, register row 3). On Rationalist ground the cull licence lists what the cull yielded, and a relic off a licensed cull is a good with a seal on its record; one off an unlicensed cull is not, and an examiner who reads it as seated may ask who licensed the beast. On Devout ground the parish enters it, and a Master relic is, in doctrine, an entry out of order carried on a person. On Reverie ground it is a claim in the docket that moves with the holder. Hand out the paper as the scene puts it in reach; two records of one relic may disagree, and both stand.

### At the table

- **Unread relics.** Apply the governor from the first scene and say so, as any cost is stated: "the relic drew a point." On the second showing, or on a reading that returns it, hand the governor line over.
- **Feeds** are charged when the relic is used or, for a per-scene feed, at the start of the wearer's first turn in the scene. Past Capacity they are Marks, and the player chooses to keep the relic seated as they choose to overdraw.
- **The phase turn.** When the phase is written on the sheets (§8), each seated relic is marked far or not. A Master relic entering its far phases inverts at once and stays inverted until unseated.
- **Breaking a socket.** *Split* or *The Join* (Paladin) landed on a piece of gear breaks the socket: the relic is unseated at once and the Capacity returns; the relic is not destroyed. Seams reads where a piece breaks (§4).
- **Non-player practitioners** carry relics on their cost files at the same Capacity cost (§7).
- **Guru relics** are campaign objects, written as Guru beasts are: one Line- or Ground-scale effect (a Line you engage loses 1 Cohesion at the start of each of its turns; readings of the relic's layer inside its ground return one grade more in facts), a Tether, and a record. The tether's Mark per phase is a standing charge (§3).
