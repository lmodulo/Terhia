**v0.1 — 25 September 2026.** Companion to Terhia-Heroes-Players-Guide.md v0.3 (PG) and Terhia-Heroes-Game-Masters-Guide.md v0.3 (GMG). Converted from Terhia-Grand-Beasts.md v0.2 (24 September 2026), the *Terhia: Chronicle* module. Built against Terhia-Canon.md v1.2: Part I (Moons and Calendar; Substrate and Nodes), Part VIII (Ecological Engine), XI.3 (Beast Register), Appendix D (prior Guru-tier events), R.8 (deferred workstreams). Mechanics only; no adventures. Every conversion decision that touches canon or the GMG is a GB-row in §5 for Joe's ratification. Canon is not edited by this file; neither is the PG or the GMG. **A table can play from the working readings as written.**

**Scope.** What a beast is and how a ground produces one (§1); the rules for running a beast at each tier in Heroes (§2); the node ceilings of record and four worked emergences, one per rank (§3); the tables for generating a beast and the procedure for converting any Chronicle v0.2 entry (§4); the register (§5). GMG §6 (Grand Beasts as bosses) is the rule of record for beasts in Heroes; this module carries the formula, the generation procedure, the ceilings, the entry format, and the worked entries that GMG §6.1 says are kept here. Where this file and GMG §6 differ, the GMG stands until the GB-row is ratified.

**What changed from Chronicle v0.2.** The formula, the grid, the tier table, the node rule, the frequency of tiers, the ceilings, the ethos-as-record rule, the entry designations, and the generation tables are carried over unchanged, because they are canon or read canon directly. The Chronicle's beast blocks (Rank Die d4/d6/d8, Guard 12–16, Vigor 12–26) are replaced by the GMG's Boss line by tier (§2.1). The Chronicle's tier rules — Trait / Rigidity / Exploit, Enhancement / Dependency / Disruption, Dominance / Reversal, Domain / Tether / Displacement — become the GMG's four vulnerability procedures (Pattern, Dependency, Reversal, Tether) with phases, and the Chronicle's procedural detail is kept wherever it does not contradict the GMG (§2.2–2.5). Readings by layer become Readings by class (GMG §6.4). Lines become Mobs (GMG §5). The 71-entry roll of record is reduced to four worked entries, one per rank; the other 67 stay in Chronicle v0.2 and convert on demand by §4.6. Relics follow GMG §6.6 and §7.

**Status against canon.** Canon Appendix R.8 defers a full bestiary and states the constraint: species lists are against the engine's design, because creatures are environmentally produced, not designed. This module contains no species. Every entry is one emergence, on one ground, in one phase, carrying the five XI.3 fields (base animal, biome, moon, node reading, tier) and a designation in the XI.3 form (ground, descriptor, date). Each entry is a candidate XI.3 row and is not canon until entered. Beasts from the Season 1 episodes, when entered from the episode files, take precedence over any row here that shares a ground. The reading of R.8 that permits the module is GB1; reject it and the entries stand as worked examples of the generation procedure only.

**Reading order.** §1 and §2 before the first beast scene. §3.0 for the ceiling of the ground the party is on; §3.1–3.4 for the entry the session is running. §4 when a ground needs a beast this file does not hold, or when a Chronicle entry is wanted at the Heroes table.

**Vocabulary map (Chronicle → Heroes).** Favor / Hindrance → Advantage / Disadvantage. Difficulty → DC, same numbers (13 / 16 / 19 / 22 / 26 / 30; PG §2). Stuck → Rooted, or Grabbed where something holds. A Reserve check against fear → Frightened, an attack vs Ward. Notice at a Difficulty → a Notice check (Reckoning or Attunement, PG §3) at that DC. Rank Die → the tier's RD (d6 / d8 / d10 / d12; the Chronicle's beast dice were one step lower). Circle → Burst 2–3; Ground → the map; a Line at Squad scale → a Mob squad (2×5). Surprised → the ambush round (§2.4). Move in paces → Speed in squares: Chronicle Move × ¾, rounded to an even number, 6 to 10 (flight and swimming as the entry says). Layers → the class Readings of GMG §6.4. Environmental Strain → 1 Strain (§2.5).

## 1. What a beast is

### 1.1 The formula

Creature = Base Animal × Biome × Moon × Ethos (Canon Part VIII, Creature Generation). Canon states that this is not metaphorical: the four factors shape what emerges. In play the four factors are four questions the GM answers before a beast enters a scene, and the answers fill the five XI.3 fields.

1. **Base animal.** An animal of the ground. The beast is that animal with something changed by the substrate. It eats what the animal eats, dens where the animal dens, and fears what the animal fears, except where the change says otherwise. Record it; the August 2026 audit's duplicate ("Kilnback") was a register failure, and a beast without a base animal cannot be checked against a register. In Heroes the base animal also fixes what the tier line does not: Size on the grid, Speed and movement mode (swim, fly, burrow, climb), the damage kind of its natural weapon (Physical; the sub-kind — cut, pierce, crush — only matters to relics and resistances), and how the tier's damage budget is split into attacks.
2. **Biome.** The biome of the ground (Canon Part V; nine biomes, with Coastal and Mountain as overlays on top of any biome). The biome selects a row of the grid.
3. **Moon.** The phase at emergence selects the column. The cell is the ambient effect the beast expresses or was produced by. Node activity fixes the tier ceiling: no beast above the node's reading, and no Master tier from a dormant node. The GM checks the ceiling (§3.0) before placing a fight; a Sorcerer's Reading can report it (GMG §6.1).
4. **Ethos.** Canon states that the prevailing ethos of the ground shapes what emerges and does not state how. This module expresses ethos as the record: how the ground's institution files and handles the beast. On Rationalist ground (Blue Accord) a beast is a licensed cull, a contract, an invoice. On Devout ground (Gold Crown) it is a parish entry, and a Master-tier beast is, in doctrine, an entry out of order in the Lender's account (Canon Part I, the Crown's reading). On Reverie ground (Green Chimera) it is a docket item, a closure, a hunting claim that moves with the beast's range. The beast's body is the same on any ground; the paper is not (GB3). In Heroes the paper is also the contract: the poster, the reward, and the reputation track the beast enters are GMG §8's, in the register of the ground's institution.

The cell is the beast's ambient effect and the source of its trait, dependency, arena condition, or domain; GMG §9.3 gives each cell's effect at the table as a hazard, and a beast's entry says which of those effects it carries with it.

### 1.3 Tiers

The Grand Beast Tier table (Canon Part VIII), with the Heroes procedure each column becomes (GMG §6.3; §2 below). The table is the fight's structure, not flavor.

| Tier | Power source | Vulnerability origin | Vulnerability logic | Heroes procedure (§2) | Boss Vigor | Phases |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:-:|:-:|
| Novice | Amplified natural trait | Behavioral rigidity | Pattern exploitation | **Pattern**: a fixed cycle with a tell; read it, exploit it | 60 | 2 |
| Adept | Magical enhancement | Biological dependency | Substrate disruption | **Dependency**: a site on the map; deny it and the enhancement ends | 110 | 3 |
| Master | Environmental dominance | Energy inversion | Environmental reversal | **Reversal**: the arena is the beast's; reverse the condition mid-fight for a window | 450 | 4 |
| Guru | Lunar embodiment | Geographic tether | Domain displacement | **Tether**: an avatar of parts that returns; unseat three tether points across a campaign | 800 per manifestation | Repeats |

Tier is not size and not species. A hare can be Adept and a bear Novice; the tier is the node's, the animal's is only the shape. A Guru-tier beast is not an animal at all in the way the other three are: it is the moon's signature expressed through a ground, tethered to a node, and the base-animal field is usually a dispute between records (Frostvatn: the parishes say a beast, the households say the lake mother; Canon R.5 #6, not settled).

### 1.4 The node

The substrate is more responsive at nodes, fixed loci tethered to terrain features (Canon Part I). Node activity is a reading, kept in the Firstday Watch's phase ledgers; it governs what emerges and not what a practitioner pays (decided 11 September 2026; Heroes' one moon lever, GMG §9.4, is a phase rule and not a node rule). Beast tier cannot exceed the node's reading. A dormant node sustains Novice and Adept emergence and nothing above. Readings across the map have not returned to pre-1218 baseline (Canon Part IX, The Present); every ceiling in §3.0 is the Watch's figure for Velquor 1245, and a second record disagrees with it wherever one exists. Rank of relic ≤ tier of emergence ≤ node reading (GMG §7.6): a dormant-node region has no Master relics that were not carried in.

### 1.5 Frequency

How often a tier occurs, stated as the records state it:

- **Novice.** Common. A herder's count, a fire-house tally, a harbor-master's log carries several per phase on any working ground. Most are pests, prizes, or hazards of a season; most are never filed. In Heroes a Novice beast is a Hard fight for a Novice party of four (6 TP) and an Easy one for an Adept party (2 TP): the contract a Warden's board hands to a level-3 party.
- **Adept.** Uncommon. On the order of one per settlement per year, on the Ledger's cull licences for Accord territory; the Concord's dockets carry more, because a Chimera claimant reports what a Ledger examiner would not license. A Hard fight for an Adept party; Deadly at level 5.
- **Master.** Rare. Two in the Chronicle since 1145 (Shalei 1155, Mwamba 1186), each a period's largest beast event; Chronicle v0.2's roll added two standing [N], one of which is §3.3. A Master-tier beast closes a road, empties a valley, or holds a front line; its reversal is a phase-scale operation whose last act is the fight.
- **Guru.** Five Watch entries in the calendar of record (Velquor 1027, Namaris 1062, Threnis 1091, Saelura 1118, Velquor 1230). Chronicle v0.2's roll added one rising [N], which is §3.4. A Guru-tier emergence is a campaign, not an encounter.

### 1.6 Reading a beast

A beast holds information in registers like anything else, and the partition is the point: no single class's Reading returns a beast's vulnerability together with its power, and a Grand Beast's vulnerability is complete only when two schools read it together (PG §11). Readings are free — 0 Strain, no action, once per round per reader — and the exploit costs the reader's Quick, or an Action when the Quick is spent (PG §11, R5). The GM says one sentence and no more; a Reading that fails is still a result.

**GMG §6.4 is the table:** one line per class per tier. It is not copied here, so that the two files do not drift. Its shape, by tier: at Novice the Readings between them return the cycle, the tell, and the next square; at Adept, where the enhancement sits, what the body needs, and where the site is; at Master, the condition, what reverses it, where the reversal point is, and how wide the window will be; at Guru, the tether's place, the part carrying the load, and which tether point is load-bearing — and four classes fail at Guru (Apostle returns "the Debtor"; Apothecarist, Berserker, and Warlock read nothing in an avatar), which is the partial-view rule made mechanical (GMG G8, open). Each entry in §3 lists its own *Reads as* lines in that shape; a class not listed reads what §6.4 says for the tier, or "nothing here", which is a true return.

## 2. Running beasts

### 2.1 The Boss line

A Grand Beast is a Boss (GMG §4.3, §4.4) on the tier's Boss line, with the tier's vulnerability procedure and phases on top (GMG §6). These are the defaults an entry deviates from; every entry in §3 lists its own numbers.

| Tier | Guard / Ward | Attack | Vigor | Damage budget per round | Attacks per Action (each) | Tempo | Interrupts (strike) | Size · Speed |
|:--|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:--|:-:|:-:|:--|
| Novice | 16 / 15 | +4 | 60 | 16 | 2 × 6 (1d6+3) | 1 | 1 (4) | Medium or Large · 6–8 |
| Adept | 19 / 18 | +7 | 110 | 32 | 2 × 6 (1d8+2) | 2 | 1 (8) | Large · 6–8 |
| Master | 22 / 21 | +10 | 450 | 55 | 2 × 10 (2d8+1) | 2 | 2 (8) | Large or Huge · 6–8 |
| Guru | 24 body; parts 22–26 / 25 | +14 | 800 per manifestation | 90 | 2 × 11 (2d8+2) per part that attacks | 3 | 3 (8) | 4×4 or larger · 6, or 0 if the manifestation is the terrain |

- **Guard and Ward** are the tier's standard figures +2 for the Boss role (GMG §4.2, §4.3). The Guru avatar keeps the tier Guard 24 for the body and varies it by part (GMG §6.5).
- **The damage budget** is the GMG tier table's Boss figure and includes the Interrupts: Bosses make two attacks per Action, and the per-round figure is attacks × Actions + Interrupts × the tier's Interrupt strike (GMG §4.2). Dice are optional; the flat figure is the calibration. An entry may spend part of the budget on an ability instead of attacks (a roar, a drag, a burst), and says so.
- **Tempo and Interrupts** are GMG §3.2 and §6.3: beast Tempo Novice 1 · Adept 2 · Master 2 · Guru 3; Interrupts 1 / 1 / 2 / 3. A beast's Interrupts are its own — a lash, a step, a call — and are listed on the entry. They refresh at the start of the beast's turn; a Stunned Boss loses them until its next turn (GMG §10.4).
- **Shake-off, hex cap 3, Weakness, half-on-miss:** as every Boss (GMG §4.4). A beast's listed Weakness is its tier's vulnerability logic.
- **No Strain, no Marks.** A beast's power is paid for by the ground (its trait, its dependency, its arena, its domain), not by its body, and the tier's vulnerability procedure is where the bill is presented. A beast never Pushes.
- **The base animal sets the rest.** Size (a lynx is Medium; a tiger Large; a python of no recorded size Huge), Speed and mode, the natural weapon's sub-kind, and which of its Actions are attacks. Nothing about the animal changes the line's Vigor or Guard: a Novice hare and a Novice ox are both Vigor 60, and the ox is Large.
- **Two or more beasts of one entry** act on one Initiative. A second beast of the same entry in one fight is a second Boss for the budget (GMG §4.6).

### 2.2 Novice — Pattern

*Power: an amplified natural trait. Vulnerability: behavioral rigidity. Logic: pattern exploitation.*

- **Trait.** One natural trait raised past the animal's range — speed, concealment, endurance, a bite, a display, footing, a coat — written as a Boss ability or a passive on the block. It is always on. It is what the big turn of the cycle does.
- **Pattern.** The beast runs a fixed **3-turn cycle** (stalk → charge → recover, or the entry's own) with a **tell** the turn before its big attack (GMG §6.3). The cycle is the Rigidity: one thing the beast cannot vary — a route, an hour, a target, a return, a refusal — and the entry names it. It is an animal's, not a flaw designed in.
- **Reading it.** The pattern is *read* when any of these is true: a Reading returns it (any class; the Hexblade's returns the tell a turn early); the party has watched two full cycles; or a record in the party's hands states it — a herder's count, a fire-house tally, a hunter's habit (GB5). Once read, the **exploit** is on: on the recover turn, attacks against the beast have Advantage and its Guard is −4. A Novice beast whose pattern is not read is fought on its Boss line, and a party that fights it that way spends its Vigor to learn nothing.
- **The hunt's end.** Each entry lists one way the pattern ends the emergence without a kill — the Rigidity used against the animal, at a listed cost in time or work. It counts as the vulnerability logic executed for the outcome line (§2.7): the Trait's material is left, and the animal goes back to being one. A hunt: the party's first job is to watch.
- **Phase 2** (half Vigor): the cycle changes and a new tell must be read; the map changes with it (the entry says how); one new ability; one new Reading line. Damage carries over.

### 2.3 Adept — Dependency

*Power: magical enhancement. Vulnerability: biological dependency. Logic: substrate disruption.*

- **Enhancement.** While the Dependency stands the beast has **one resistance and one ability** beyond any animal's range (GMG §6.3), stated as a Boss ability at the scale an Adept technique would have (one target, or Burst 2–3). It is always on while the Dependency stands.
- **Dependency.** One thing on the ground the enhancement draws on: a den-site whose reading is off, a spring, a stand of trees, a carcass ground, a lick, a roost, a crust. The beast returns to it; the animal in it needs it. On the map it is a **site** — a square or a 2×2, with a 30-Vigor object at it (a root-mouth, a den wall, a crust, a nest) — and the entry says where. The Dependency is *found* by the Readings the entry names (the Apothecarist names the biological dependency; the Elementalist which element it needs; the Warden its den and route; the Paladin the site's break threshold; GMG §6.4), or by a record that already holds it. Finding the beast is not finding the Dependency.
- **Deny it.** Two Actions at the site and a check vs **DC 16** (any attribute the fiction supports: Frame to tear a root-mouth, Attunement to turn a spring, Reckoning to channel it), or breaking the 30-Vigor object there (GMG §6.3). Denied, the resistance and the ability end for the rest of the fight. A Disruption executed while the beast is present is executed under attack. **Denied before the fight** — downtime work at the site over the time the entry states (a day's digging, a felled stand, a cut sandbar), or a legal act on Chimera ground that moves people off it — the beast is met without them from the first round; it still goes to the site at half Vigor, because it does not know (GB6).
- **Phase 2** (half Vigor): the beast moves to the Dependency and fights at it; the entry gives it something at the site (a regeneration, an ambush, the numbers) that also ends when the site is denied. **Phase 3** (quarter Vigor): damage ×1.25, Speed +2, no more Interrupts — it has stopped thinking. It does not know that its enhancement has ended until it uses it.

### 2.4 Master — Reversal

*Power: environmental dominance. Vulnerability: energy inversion. Logic: environmental reversal.*

- **The arena is the beast's.** Its ground expresses the cell at strength wherever the beast is: an environmental condition (fog, flooded ground, heat, ice, dark, cover, smoke, sand) that grants it **+1 Interrupt** and **one resistance** and puts a **hazard on the party** (GMG §6.3), listed on the entry as standing conditions on everyone on the map. Where the cell is an ambush (chaparral × Threnis, predator magnification), the arena includes the **ambush round**: in the first round of any engagement on its ground the beast acts first, from Hidden, whatever the Initiative — the Warden's *The Unclosing Eye* excepted (GB7). A Master beast in its arena cannot be killed by a party of its rank in the rounds its Vigor allows, and is not meant to be; it can be fought by attrition only at the cost §2.6 gives.
- **Energy inversion.** The condition draws on the cell. When the cell turns — the phase changes, the fuel is gone, the water comes back — the draw reverses and the beast is at its weakest. The entry states the inversion as a condition of the map and names the **kind** the beast is Vulnerable to in it; it is the kind the arena's resistance was.
- **Reversal.** The party must **reverse the condition mid-fight**: a map task of **3 steps in Phase 1, 2 in Phase 2, 1 in Phase 3** — each step an Action at a point on the map plus a check vs **DC 18**, or a structure break (GMG §6.3, §10.8). Completing the task opens a **window of 2 rounds**: Guard −4, Vulnerable to the named kind, no Interrupts, and no concealment the arena gave. Each phase the beast re-asserts the condition and changes the map (water rises, a ridge falls, the fog thickens, the scrub closes), and the next task is shorter. **Phase 4** (quarter Vigor) has no reversal: the window is permanent and its damage is ×1.25.
- **The operation and its last act.** The Chronicle's phase-scale operation — a counter-burn across a hill, an oasis collapsed, a waterhole raised a mile away, burn rights lent under fire law — is what puts the steps on the map: it is prepared in play, priced in labor-days, in contracts, in the Concord's docket, or in a Guru's Marks, and the fight is its last act (GB8). The Reversal is *identified* by the Readings the entry names (Elementalist: the condition and what reverses it; Necromancer: where the arena's deaths cluster; Artificer: the mechanism; Paladin: the structure to break; GMG §6.4), by a Join, or by a record: the Shalei hunters of 1156 had the oasis from three caravan ledgers, not from a Reading.
- **Left on its ground.** A Master beast does not leave its ground; it dies there or the party does. A party that leaves (GMG §4.7, the leaving procedure) finds it at full Vigor and in Phase 1 at the next phase turn, because the ground restores it; inside a phase it keeps its wounds (GB9).

### 2.5 Guru — Tether

*Power: lunar embodiment. Vulnerability: geographic tether. Logic: domain displacement.*

- **Domain.** A Ground (a valley, a lake basin, a forest reach, a harbor approach) that expresses the cell at full strength for as long as the embodiment stands. The entry lists the Domain's effects at Ground scale as standing conditions. Three apply in every Domain (GB10): a Novice of the moon's own school pays **1 Strain** at the start of each turn in which it Reads inside the Domain, because the register is louder than the reader; every Reading of that school inside the Domain returns **two sentences** instead of one, the second precise; and lesser emergences inside a Domain are **one tier above their entry, to Master**, whatever the homeland's ceiling says elsewhere — the Domain is the reading.
- **The avatar.** A Guru tier is not fought to a kill. If it presents a body — the parishes at Frostvatn said it did — that body is the **avatar**: 4×4 or larger, Speed 6 or 0 if the manifestation is the terrain, Vigor 800 per manifestation, Tempo 3, Interrupts 3, built of **3–5 parts**, each with its own Guard (body 24; a limb 22; a core 26) and **part Vigor = 800 ÷ (parts + 1)** (GMG §6.5). Damage to a part is damage to the avatar. Destroying a part removes one listed ability, grants a Reading of the tether to whoever destroyed it, and does not reduce the avatar's Vigor below the parts still standing. It can be climbed (DC 20 with a Move; attacks on the part stood on have Advantage; attacked only by Interrupts), blinded per part, and moved 1 pace per 50 damage by a Ground-scale Force or Storm effect. **Manifestation destroyed** at 0 Vigor: it dissolves for the phase and **returns at the next phase of its moon** (or the next Firstday, on the GM's clock) at full, minus parts permanently removed. Killing the body is a record of a body killed and nothing else. This is the rule for "could not be killed" (Canon Part I).
- **Tether.** A location: the node anchor by which the embodiment holds the Ground — a basin, a range, a reef, an oasis, a river bend. It is what a Sorcerer's Guru-grade Reading returns as the two editable values; it is what a keeper's closure holds empty; it is the headwater the Iselva diversion cut. It is never the beast's body.
- **Tether points.** Unseating the tether is a **campaign objective**, not a fight: **three tether points** on a regional map, each unseated by a Guru-scale action — a Guru ability at Ground scale, a season's engineering work, a joint operation, a treaty that moves people (GMG §6.3). Each point unseated removes one part permanently from the next manifestation and one ability with it. The two methods in the record are both here: *engineering* (Accord, Frostvatn 1230–32: divert a headwater, cut a seawall, dry a basin) and *closure* (Chimera, Marama 1091: a keeper's closure held empty across two phase turns and renewed at each; the Watch records the reading fallen and does not know why). **The third point is always the last, and it is always a Join at DC 30** at the anchor (PG §2, "beyond one hand"; GMG §10.3): two schools, each on its own register of the anchor — Structure and Element for an engineering displacement; Life and Structure, or Life and Boundary, for a closure. All three unseated: the Domain collapses with the avatar (Frostvatn, Velquor 1232). Nothing else displaces a Guru tier; damage does not, and no single school does (GB11).

### 2.6 Numbers, calibration, and attrition

- **Herds, packs, swarms, colonies.** Where the base animal comes in numbers, the entry runs them as a **Mob** (GMG §5) in a sliding block (animals reshape freely and ignore the gap rule), at the unit the count gives — band 5, squad 10, platoon 40 — and at the **drill of the beast's tier** (Novice tier, Novice drill; Adept, Adept; Master, Master), Cohesion 6 / 9 / 12 per member (GB12). Where a lead animal exists (a sow, a bull, a pack leader, the old male), it is the Boss on the tier's Boss line and the numbers are its Mob: the Ulvskov pack of GMG §11.4 is the model. Where there is no leader (a swarm, a colony's soldiers, a roost), the Mob is the whole beast, the tier's procedure applies to it — its Dependency denied removes the drill's Cohesion bonus and one listed ability — and it breaks at 0 Cohesion into nothing worth counting. Areas and DoTs deal double to Cohesion (GMG §5.4), which is what a caster is for against a swarm.
- **Threat Points.** A beast is a Boss: **6 TP** at its tier (GMG §4.6); its Mob adds the unit's TP (band 2, squad 4, platoon 10). Cross-tier as the GMG: ÷3 one tier down, ×3 one tier up, and **two tiers or more above is not a stand-up fight** — the GM says the tier out loud when the first Reading lands, and the leaving procedure (GMG §4.7) is the scene. Within a tier, a level-5 or level-9 party is at the bottom of its band: treat Hard as Deadly.
- **Attrition.** A Novice beast can be killed on its line without its pattern read; an Adept beast without its Dependency denied; a Master beast — 450 Vigor, a resistance, three Interrupts, the arena's hazard every round — only by a party that accepts a fight of ten rounds or more with no window. Each is legal, and each leaves less (§2.7). A Guru avatar destroyed by attrition returns.
- **Stunned.** A Boss, once per phase; a second Stun in the same phase is Slowed (GMG §10.4). *Time Debt* once per encounter.

### 2.7 What an emergence leaves

A relic (PG §15.2; GMG §7). The tier's outcome line is where it comes from, and how the beast was beaten is what makes it separable from the body (GMG §6.6):

| Tier | Outcome line | Leaves, if the vulnerability logic was executed | Leaves, if killed by attrition alone |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Novice | **Trait** | 1 Novice relic (Rigidity governor) — the pattern exploited or the hunt ended by it | Nothing usable; a hide |
| Adept | **Dependency** | 1 Adept relic (Dependency governor), from the site, which is still there after the beast is an animal again, + 1 Novice relic | 1 Novice relic |
| Master | **Dominance** | 1 Master relic (Inversion governor) + 1 Adept relic; the arena's condition ends for a season | 1 Adept relic; the condition persists |
| Guru | **Tether** | 1 named Guru relic (Tether governor) + 2 Master relics at domain collapse; the node reading falls one step | Per manifestation destroyed: 1 Master relic; the avatar returns |

The relic's attribute is taken from the GMG §7.5 catalogue at the tier's magnitude, from the beast's own line — its trait, enhancement, dominance, or tether — and its moon is the emergence's. Entries in §3 carry one suggested relic each in the GMG §7.9 form; the GM writes the rest from the entry when the outcome line is reached. The GM never rolls for relics; the outcome line is the roll. The registry consequences are GMG §7.8: Accord residue is declared cargo; Velquor-line relics are contraband on Crown ground; a Threnis relic on Chimera ground belongs to the ground.

### 2.8 Records

Every count a beast entry carries names its counter (Canon Discipline 5). The parish counts what it tithes, the Ledger what it licenses, the Concord what a claimant reports, a caravan house what it lost. Two records of one beast disagree, and both are handed out as the scene puts them in reach. A beast's date is the date of the record, not of the emergence; the Watch's phase entry is a reading, not a sighting. In Heroes the record is also a route to the vulnerability (§2.2, §2.4): a party that reads the paper before it reads the beast has done the first job.

### 2.9 Entry format

Each entry gives, in order: **designation** (ground, descriptor, phase and year of record) — tier · base animal · biome × moon: the cell · node reading (record) · ground's institution. Then *Ecology* (what the cell changed, range, season, sign); *Boss block* (GMG §4.1: Size, Speed, Tempo, Guard / Ward, Vigor, attack, attacks, abilities, resist / vulnerable, Interrupts, Weakness); the tier's procedure lines (Pattern · tell · exploit · hunt's end; Dependency · deny; Arena · inversion · reversal; Domain · avatar · tether · tether points); *Phases* (GMG §6.2); *Reads as* (one line per class that plausibly reads it); *Outcome line* (what it leaves, with one relic written); *Records*. The designation is the XI.3 name field; nothing in it is a coined word.

At the bend, after 1 and 2: *Read the Living* at Guru grade (the reach entire, and what it needs, which is to be left) joined with *Read Structure* at Guru grade (the anchor's two values, and which to unseat); or *Read the Living* joined with *Read the Crossing* (every crossing on the reach since the turn, which is the account the keepers' closure is keeping). One roll, the higher contributor's, vs 30; 2 Strain each; once per encounter. The Domain collapses on the success. Nothing is killed.

*Reads as.* Sorcerer: the tether's coordinates — the bend. Elementalist: which moon it embodies — Threnis; what sustained work would hold it — the river held fast through the bend. Apostle: returns "the Debtor" — a doctrinal problem, not a target; the shrine-keeper's letter is this Reading, unanswered. Apothecarist: the avatar fails — not a living system; the reach, read at Ground scale, answers: what it needs is to be left (GB17). Necromancer: the crossing count at the node — every crossing on the reach since the turn. Warden: the Domain's edge — the landing's seaward side, and the first falls. Hexblade: the phase in which it is thinnest — Saelura or Orrivane, the far phases from Threnis, when its Interrupts are 2. Vanguard: which part has reach — the root-reaches, 3. Paladin: the part carrying the load — the keeper's face; break it first. Berserker: fails — the gate reads nothing above Master. Artificer: which tether point is load-bearing — the slow water. Warlock: fails — nothing to dose.

*Outcome line.* Tether. Domain collapsed: 1 named Guru relic — **Waimotu reach-root (Guru, named)** · Origin: Waimotu reach, rising — Threnis 1244 · Awanui · Guru · Threnis · Line: Tether · *Attribute:* Healing dealt, +1 RD per heal · *Alteration:* a bounded piece of Morphic Borrowing — **Borrowed reach** · Action · once per encounter · 2 rounds · your Strike or Core has reach 3 and a hit Grabs; an Apothecarist uses it at 4 Strain (GB18); anyone else only as a Push, 1 Mark, once per round (R3) · *Governor:* Tether — works on the reach, or for seven days after a Breather taken at the bend, or in the hands of its bonded holder · Seats in a Guru socket or the Trinket · Bond: the tether's — and 2 Master relics; the node reading falls one step and does not return to baseline. Per manifestation destroyed: 1 Master relic; the avatar returns. On Chimera ground the relic belongs to the ground, and the council's keeper decides; a Guru relic that leaves the reach is filed by the Ledger at Puketai the day it is seen (GMG §7.7).

*Records.* The council's closure. The Tithe's letter. The Ledger's estimate, which stops at the pilot's last mark. The Sedge oral record, asked, says the reach has always had a keeper and now it is awake, which the Ledger classifies as anecdotal and the pilots decline to comment on. Three accounts of one reading; the rule for episodes is that no one settles it (Canon XI.6, lunar embodiments vs gods).

*Budget.* Not a stand-up fight for anyone below Guru. A Guru party of four against one manifestation: 6 TP at Guru, and the fight is 3–4 rounds at full damage, 6–7 at the table, once a phase, for a season; the tether points are the arc and their cost ledger is the campaign.

## 4. Generating a beast

For a ground this file does not cover, a new phase, or a Chronicle v0.2 entry wanted at the Heroes table. The steps are the Generation Procedure (Canon Part VIII) as dice; every result is a candidate XI.3 row and goes in the session's ratification register.

### 4.1 Count, by ground and phase

| Tier | Roll | Condition |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Novice | 1d4 + 1 standing on any working ground; 1d3 new per phase | — |

### 4.2 Moon at emergence

d5 (a d10 halved, rounded up): 1 Velquor, 2 Saelura, 3 Orrivane, 4 Namaris, 5 Threnis. Master tier: 1–8 on a d12 is Threnis; 9–12 the others in order. The moon fixes the column; read the cell; the cell is the beast's ambient effect and the source of its trait, dependency, arena, or domain. The moon is also the relic's moon, and the phase in which the school of its moon has Strain Capacity +2 (GMG §9.4).

### 4.3 Base animal

The fauna of the ground. These are examples of what lives there, not species of beast; any animal of the biome and the culture's daily life serves, and the mounts and draft animals of Canon Part I are fauna too. The animal sets Size and Speed on the Boss line (§2.1); it never sets Vigor or Guard.

### 4.4 Prompts by tier

Roll d6 or choose. Each prompt is written as the Heroes procedure wants it.

**Novice — the Pattern.**

|:-:|:--|:--|:--|

**Master — the arena.** The condition is the cell at strength wherever the beast is: write it as one benefit to the beast (+1 Interrupt and a resistance are fixed; add concealment, a movement mode, or the ambush round as the cell gives it) and one hazard on the party from GMG §9.3's cell line. The inversion is the cell's opposite phase — Saelura for most, Namaris for a chaparral or grassland cover, Velquor for a herd-beast — and the Vulnerable kind is the resistance's. The reversal is the operation that produces the inversion on purpose — a burn, a flood, a well, a closure — prepared in play, with three points on the map for the fight's 3-2-1 task, each a DC 18 check or a structure of 15–40 Vigor. Write the four phases from the operation's cost: what the beast takes back at each.

**Guru — the Domain.** Write the Watch entry first and the rules second. The Domain is a Ground; its standing conditions are the cell at full expression plus the three of §2.5. The avatar is 3–5 parts from the terrain the tether is (a shelf, a reach, a crown, a face), part Vigor 800 ÷ (parts + 1), one ability per part. The tether is a terrain feature's node, never the body. The three tether points: one from the record's method (closure or engineering), one from the other, and the last a Join at DC 30 at the anchor. A Guru is a season; its cost ledger is the arc.

### 4.5 Records

Every beast has two counts. Roll d6 for the second counter: 1 a parish or precinct roll; 2 a Ledger licence or assessment; 3 a Concord docket or a keeper's closure; 4 a caravan house, timber house, or fish-house ledger; 5 a moot, council, or lineage count; 6 the Watch's phase entry, which is a reading and not a sighting. The first count is the ground's institution's, and in Heroes it is the contract (GMG §8.2).
