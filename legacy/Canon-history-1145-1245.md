# Terhia: A Survey of the Century, 1145–1245

> Compiled against the Firstday Watch calendar of record, cross-checked with Cobalt Ledger registries, Gilt Chancel accounts, and Steel Quorum after-action files. Period covered: 1 Velquor 1145 through the present year, 1245. Tribal-territory events rely on Bramble Concord minutes and Sedge Circle oral record, which the Ledger classifies as anecdotal. They are included anyway. A century of history written only from what the Ledger will certify would be half a century of history.

---

## The World in 1145

The century opens on a settlement already a generation old. The Concordat of Pingyeh (1146, ratifying terms negotiated the prior year) closed the last of the border wars of the 1130s and fixed the institutional map that has held, with adjustments, ever since: twelve homelands, three charters.

Alignment is charter, not uniformity. Every homeland holds practitioners of every school, adherents of every ethos, and at least one faction working against its own government's interest. The Unsworn held simultaneous contracts with all three institutions in 1145 and hold them still.

The Gold Crown's pontifical seat sat then, as now, at the citadel of Qasr-Al-Rimal in Bahr-Al-Asra, its treasury and land titles administered by the Gilt Chancel, its ritual heartland the temple precincts of Tepemitla in Teocalli. By convention dating to the citadel's consecration, each Sacrist-General takes the name al-Rimal on elevation, whatever their birth culture. The Blue Accord's examination boards convened at Baishui in Zhenshui under a First Examiner seated by open merit cycle. The Green Chimera had then, and has now, no head — the Bramble Concord had not yet been chartered, and inter-tribal dispute in 1145 was settled claim by claim, season by season.

## The Ledger Peace, 1145–1163

The Concordat's most durable product was not a border. It was a document regime.

In 1149 the Cobalt Ledger completed the General Register: the first full census of licensed practitioners across all Accord territory, every entry recording school, rank, and measured casting cost. Crown territory adopted a parallel registry within a decade, administered through the Indigo Assay. Chimera territory refused, and refuses. The Register converted magic from a talent into an accountable asset, and the institutions spent the next hundred years arguing over the accounting.

Trade grew on two competing systems. The Echo Portal network — Accord-chartered, Arcane-staffed, priced by the Portal Compact of 1157 — moved passengers and light cargo between homeland seats. Airship guilds, unchartered and undercutting portal tolls, moved bulk freight. The Accord's Manifest Rule (1159), requiring registered cargo declarations on all portal traffic, was written to tax the portals and instead pushed the profitable contraband onto the airships and the overland Threnis-territory routes. The smuggling economy that would consolidate as the Black Meridian was priced into existence by a customs regulation.

The period's largest recorded beast event was modest by later standards: a Master-tier burrow-dominant emerged in the Shalei Desert during the Threnis phase of 1155, closed the Shakou caravan road for a season, and was displaced the following Saelura phase when hunters collapsed its oasis dependency — a standard environmental reversal, executed by contract, invoiced to three merchant houses.

## The Purge Years, 1163–1169

On 41 Namaris 1163, Sacrist-General Harun al-Rimal was killed at Tepemitla, struck down on the temple causeway during the Namaris-phase rites, in front of roughly four thousand witnesses who saw nothing. The blade was never recovered.

What happened in the following hour is the most litigated event of the century. A Necrotic Master attending the rites unlicensed performed a boundary reading at the scene. A Necrotic Guru — name struck from every registry, identity still disputed — held the crossing open long enough for the dying Sacrist-General to speak. Chancel witnesses recorded the words. The words named an under-treasurer of the Gilt Chancel.

The Crown's public position, held to this day: testimony obtained across the death boundary is heresy compounding heresy, inadmissible in any court, sacred or civil. Basira al-Rimal stood on the causeway that day; the Chancel's own minutes place her among those who heard the naming. What the testimony threatened needs no speculation: in the Crown's holiest precinct, during its own rites, an unlicensed practitioner of the black moon had produced the only account of the killing that anyone believed. The Crown's private position can be inferred from what followed. The under-treasurer was dead within the year, officially of fever. And beginning in 1164, under Sacrist-General Basira al-Rimal, Crown territory conducted the systematic expulsion of every Ethosless practitioner within its borders — the Purge of the Gold Crown, 1164–1166. Necrotic practitioners, licensed or not, along with Hexblades and Warlocks of no institutional charter, were stripped of residence, property, and in documented cases, life. Crown records list 11,000 expulsions. Sedge Circle oral record, which received many of the refugees, puts the figure at three times that.

The Purge's consequences outlived its targets. The refugee flow moved south and east through tribal territory, and by 1167 the smuggling networks it traveled had consolidated under a single operating discipline: movement timed to Velquor's phase, routes through Threnis territory that the tribes taxed but did not report. The Black Meridian dates its coherence as a faction to these years. So does the Off-Ledger's transformation from a scattering of unlicensed practitioners into a deliberate inversion of the registry system. The Crown, purging its own shadow, gave the shadow structure.

## The Long Frost, 1169–1176

Guru Aslaug of Stenhavn had kept the harbor ice-free for thirty-one years. The Ledger's cost file on her is a complete record of the Elemental progression: fatigue notations from the 1140s, muscle-failure accommodations from the 1150s, organ-strain waivers signed annually from 1161, each one a decision to continue. She collapsed at the seawall on 63 Velquor 1169 and the harbor froze within the phase.

Stenhavn was Grimskov's grain port. Five winters, 1169–1174, the ice held and the imports failed, and the Kaldmark garrison spent more of those winters managing bread riots than the frontier. Grimskov pressed claims on Svarthavet fishing waters that Isavík considered its own; two fleet actions in 1172 killed several dozen sailors and settled nothing; only the Steel Quorum, selling identical blockade doctrine to both sides, profited. The Stenhavn tide log for those winters survives in the Ledger's provincial files. The entry for the morning after Aslaug died reads, in full: *no reading taken*. For thirty-one years the line had been a depth and a temperature; for the next five it is one word, *ice*, four hundred times a year, in the harbor-master's hand. The wider lesson was the one every chancery drew at once: a single Elemental Guru had been load-bearing infrastructure for an entire homeland's food supply, and no one had priced the risk.

The Guru Escrow Convention of 1174 was the repricing. Harbor-grade Elemental Gurus — there were four alive at signing — were registered as shared infrastructure, leased through the Accord under terms all three institutions countersigned, their deployment a matter of treaty rather than ownership. It remains the only document the Crown, the Accord, and a quorum of Chimera claimants have all signed. Stenhavn reopened under a leased Guru in 1175. The grain price took another year to follow.

## The Antler War, 1178–1189

The Crown came out of the Frost years financially strained and doctrinally hard, and under Basira al-Rimal's late seat it pushed the Namaris rites outward — new shrines in the borderlands where Oasira's grasslands run against tribal Threnis territory. The tribes read shrine construction as territorial claim, because it was. Shrines burned in 1178 and 1179. Crown reprisal columns crossed into unceded land in 1180. By Saelura 1181 it was a war.

It was a war neither side could win on the ground it was fought on. Crown paladin columns held anything flat and lost everything wooded; Reverie rangers and berserker bands made the Mwamba foothills impassable and could not take a walled town. Ash-and-Antler, offered Crown contracts at triple rate, refused them and spent the war warding the Accord's frontier instead — a refusal the compact still cites in its charter renewals. The Unsworn took contracts from both belligerents, which produced the war's strangest hour: at Marendi in 1184, two Unsworn companies found themselves fielded on opposite sides of the same engagement, and per compact rules, stood down in view of both armies. The battle dissolved around them. The Marendi Standstill has been cited in every mercenary contract negotiation since.

The war was ended by neither army. In the Threnis phase of 1186, with node activity along the Mwamba Range at the highest reading then on record, the forested foothills produced an apex emergence — a Master-tier predator that broke a Crown column and a tribal muster in the same eight days and held the ground between them. Both commands withdrew. Both chanceries discovered, reading the same Quorum assessment, that they had been paying for a front line a single animal could hold better than either of them.

The Treaty of Tinwara (1187) followed within the year. Its terms built two factions that persist: the Namarite Tithe, by which the Crown maintains its shrines in Chimera territory under treaty and collects along the Namaris circuit rather than holding ground; and the Bramble Concord, chartered as the standing negotiation between tribal claimants that the war had improvised. Ash-and-Antler led the cull of the Mwamba apex in 1188, executing the environmental reversal across a full phase turn. The Crown, for its part, drew an internal lesson from a war begun over rites: the Edict of Amilpan (1189) proscribed reverie practice throughout Crown territory outright — the heresy nearest its own practice, and therefore, in the Edict's own language, the least tolerable.

## The Yunmen Lag and the Manifest War, 1196–1205

The portal network's traffic peaked every Orrivane phase, when Arcane casting cost ran lowest and the fare schedules followed. On 17 Orrivane 1198, the station at Yunmen ran a dilation fault. The presiding Arcane Guru, working from inside the bounded volume, desynchronized: travelers stepped out of the Yunmen gate as much as nine days out of true, the Guru was retired with an identity-time fracture the Ledger's own examiners could not fully map, and two attending Masters developed memory dissonance within the year. First Examiner Feng Zhao, who had personally certified the Yunmen station, resigned before the audit finished.

The Accord suspended the entire network — the Interdiction, 1198–1201 — and reopened it under the exterior-placement doctrine that has governed Guru-rank casting since: the caster stands outside the edited volume, without exception, whatever the cost in precision. The doctrine is written in the dry procedural language of a station manual. Every line of it was paid for at Yunmen.

Three years of dead portals made the airship guilds rich and the caravan towns — Shakou, Yunmen itself, the Bahr Sufra crossings — richer. When the network reopened under new tariffs, the guilds declined to surrender the traffic, and the dispute ran hot from 1203 to 1205: embargoes, seized cargoes, manifests forged and counter-forged, and one true shooting incident at Shakoui harbor in 1204, when Accord customs cutters fired on a guild freighter and killed nine crew. The Ledger Accords of 1205 settled it as an accounting matter, which it always was — a unified manifest across portal and airship traffic, with tolls pegged to the moon phase, tracking the casting cost of the infrastructure itself. Freight in Terhia has been priced by the sky ever since.

## The Baishui Poisoning and the Second Register, 1211–1214

First Examiner Wen Suyin died at her own examination table at Baishui on 12 Orrivane 1211, mid-certification, of a Warlock-grade contact poison worked into the document wax. The Ledger's investigation remains, by its own classification, open.

It is open because of a rule the Ledger will not bend. A Necrotic boundary reading of the death — performed competently, offered formally, corroborated in its incidental details — was refused as evidence on the standing doctrine that what cannot be measured is anecdotal. The institution that determines truth declined the only testimony available, and has spent thirty-five years unable to name a killer while insisting the method that might name one does not meet standard. The Off-Ledger was blamed by proclamation. No individual was ever charged.

What the Accord could not solve it regulated. The Second Register (1212–1214) forced universal re-licensure: fresh cost audits at every rank, alchemy-handling certifications, poison-precursor controls, and enforcement powers for registry officials that the original General Register never contemplated. For most practitioners, the fresh audit was the first formal measurement of what their work had already taken — the tremor, the scar tissue, the early aging read aloud across an examination table by a stranger with a form. The files from those two years preserve the same notation, page after page, in the space reserved for the examinee's remarks: *declined to review results*. The compliance regime the present generation of practitioners works under — and the unlicensed evade — is the Baishui poisoning's longest consequence.

## The Node Decade, 1218–1232

From roughly 1218, the substrate itself became the story. Firstday Watch phase ledgers — kept unbroken since before the Concordat — begin recording anomalies in that year and never stop: tidal distortion in Svarthavet exceeding Velquor norms, the Tienhu fog carrying light it should not carry, Saelura-phase healing rates in the Awanui rainforest running past anything in the record. Node activity was rising across the map, and the ecology answered.

Off Puketai in the Threnis phase of 1224, the reef came coordinated. Not a beast — a biome effect at full expression: reef intelligence across a harbor's whole approach, channels closing against unwelcome hulls. Tangata navigators, working with Sedge Circle initiates, negotiated the Puketai Passage Agreements — standing terms of transit observed to this day, renewed each Threnis phase. The Accord's formal position is that one cannot hold treaty with a coral formation, and its merchant captains observe the Agreements to the letter.

Then Frostvatn. On 1 Velquor 1230 — the longest night of the year, at the strongest hour of the black moon's phase — a Guru-tier manifestation rose from Lake Frostvatn under the Hrafnrygg range: lunar embodiment, Velquor's own signature, tethered to the lake basin. Nothing in living memory matched it and the old records that did were not reassuring. It could not be killed; Guru-tier vulnerability is geographic, not biological. Isavík's northern valleys emptied. Hvítadal stood abandoned for two winters.

The response was the largest joint operation of the century, and the first since the Escrow Convention to put all three institutions in one command tent: Steel Quorum planning purchased jointly, Crown Divine support out of Isavík, Accord Arcane doctrine, Chimera wardens and Ash-and-Antler compacts holding the ground approaches, Unsworn companies on escort under a single consolidated contract — the compact's first and only. The work itself was engineering: two years diverting the Iselva headwaters and unseating the basin's substrate anchor, displacing the tether rather than fighting the beast. The manifestation collapsed with its domain in Velquor 1232. The operation's cost ledger is exact and canonical in the registries: one Elemental Guru dead of collapse at the diversion works, one Arcane Guru retired with identity-time fracture, one Divine Guru who transferred what the Assay certified as thirty-one years of her own span into the frostbitten survivors of the first winter's evacuation, and who died in 1238 at the age of fifty-four, of old age.

## The Benefice Crisis and the Disputed Seat, 1226–1239

The Crown's troubles in the same decade were fiscal before they were sacred. In 1226 the Indigo Assay, executing its charter, voided a cluster of miracle claims in Oasira — healings attributed to Divine attunement that examination showed to be staged, well-intentioned, or simply false. Fraud voids benefice: the Gilt Chancel had issued paper against those miracles, pilgrimage revenue on the Tinwara circuit collapsed, and the Chancel spent 1227 and 1228 selling land title to Accord creditors to stay solvent. The Crown kept faith with its own auditors at the price of its own treasury, which is either the most principled or the most expensive decision in its modern history, depending on which chancery is asked.

The bill came due at succession. Sacrist-General Sunniva al-Rimal died in 1236 after a thirty-two-year seat, and the leading candidate's claim rested on an authenticated healing miracle from the Frostvatn years. Assay re-examination found the healing genuine but the attribution false — the work of a contracted Divine Guru, presented as the candidate's own. Genuine grace, fraudulent claim; benefice void. Two years of contested elevation, an Assay under siege from the faction it had just unmade, and a Chancel with no money to fund a schism produced the compromise of 1239: Citlali al-Rimal, Teocalli-born, elevated with the explicit endorsement of the Assay whose ruling had cleared her path. She holds the seat in the present year.

## The Present, 1240–1245

The century closes on a world more documented, more audited, and less settled than the one it opened on.

The Second Register regime is at its strictest enforcement in thirty years, and the Off-Ledger and the Black Meridian are, by the Ledger's own confidential estimates, at their largest. The airship guilds are petitioning for full Accord charter, which would end a hundred years of profitable irregularity, and the caravan towns are lobbying against them. The Bramble Concord's Threnis-territory dockets grow longer each cycle as node-driven range shifts move beasts, and therefore hunting claims, across every negotiated line. Firstday Watch readings have not returned to pre-1218 baseline; the standing assessment is that Frostvatn was the decade's largest expression, not its conclusion.

And one rumor persists, in the way that only officially impossible things persist. The Purge of 1164–1166 is complete in every Crown record — the Ethosless expelled, the cell structure broken, the matter closed. Yet three times in the past decade, Chancel documents have surfaced through Black Meridian channels that no expelled practitioner should have been able to reach. The Crown's position is that the Purge left nothing behind. The Crown's position on the Tepemitla testimony was also, once, that the dead do not speak.

As of 1 Velquor 1245, the three charters stand where the Concordat set them: none dominant, none failed, all three solvent by narrower margins than they publish. The substrate is more active than at any point in the calendar of record. The registries have never been fuller. The unregistered have never been more numerous.
