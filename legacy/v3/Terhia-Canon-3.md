## Design Thesis

Institutions, schools, and cultures perceive the same underlying reality differently, and none holds the whole of it. The Arcane overlay reads mathematics; Nature reads relation; Necrotic reads boundary; the Crown reads lifespan as accounting; the Ledger reads it as measured cost; cultures locate authority in seals, witnesses, inherited duty, water debt, or a navigator's word. Every addition to this canon is tested against that thesis: a new element should give some party a partial view, not a complete one.

## Canon Discipline

1. **Ratification blocks.** Pending items that other sections depend on are decided together, as a block, never left pending while dependents accumulate. The current block is Appendix R.0.
2. **Residue rule.** The world contains things with no structural explanation (Part IX, Appendix C). Residue is never retro-explained; an item that acquires a cause stops being residue and must be re-filed as canon.
3. **Asymmetry rule.** Future additions need not be balanced across cultures, homelands, or institutions. A culture may have more folklore, fewer holidays, no famous general. Balance is not a ratification criterion.
4. **Registry vocabulary.** Class and rank names are Common Tongue registry terms (Part VI, Terminology). In-world speech uses the functional terms unless a registry, court, or contract is speaking.

# Part I — The World

## Premise [C]

> Medieval Earth adjacent fantasy world. Magic replaces technology. Animal mounts vs cars, Airships vs trains, Echo Portals vs airplanes, etc. etc. No modern weaponry. The severity in cost of using magic skills are nearly equal to the power of the spell. Everyone is either born with ONE magic or martial school. Each Culture has its own langauge. There is a common tongue shared across all (mostly) cultures depending on education level.

**Airship propulsion [N].** Lift and drive are Light Arts: gas cells and engines built and maintained by Master artificers, certified under the same Ledger category as locks, lifts, and signal lines. Wind-work by Elemental practitioners supplements the engines on long hauls and is priced by moon phase like every other casting; guild ships have historically crewed it with unregistered Elementals, which is one reason the Off-Ledger has never lacked employment and one reason the Second Register's inspectors board airships first. ## Moons and Calendar [C]

> Years are 400 days long. Each moon has a dominant phase lasting 80 days. The first day of each year is the longest night of the year, during Velquor's phase. Dates are written as: `48 Velquor 1242` — 48th day of Velquor's phase, year 1242. Moon cycles as such: Velquor → Saelura → Orrivane → Namaris → Threnis

| Moon | Color | School | Institution |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Velquor | Black | Necrotic | Ethosless |
| Saelura | Red | Elemental | *shared* |
| Orrivane | Purple | Arcane | Blue Accord |
| Namaris | Gold | Divine | Gold Crown |
| Threnis | Green | Nature | Green Chimera |

**Calendar consequences established elsewhere in canon (gathered here):** casting cost runs lowest in a school's own phase (portal fares peak-traffic in Orrivane; "an Orrivane price" is cheap, "a Velquor price" is not); the Firstday Watch keeps 1 Velquor as the observance of record; the Namaris circuit governs Crown tithe collection; Threnis-phase claims govern tribal territory; Saelura-phase well work is when desert oases stabilize; five phase-turn days are market truces (Renlei) and Umutu age-set cohorts are cut by birth phase.

**Day subdivision [N].** The day is divided into twenty-four hours under Cobalt Ledger measurement convention ("Ledger hours"), kept by registries, examination boards, and portal schedules, and reckoned from midnight. Cultures keep their own working divisions — watches at sea, board bells at Baishui, draw-hours at Unsaan wells — and convert to Ledger hours only when a document requires it. The "3:47" in the Necrotic emotional payload is a Ledger statistic: the registry records time of death for every filed crossing, and practitioners know the regional average because the Ledger publishes it. The figure stands.

## Substrate and Nodes [N]

The terms *substrate*, *node*, and *node activity* are used throughout the ratified ecological engine and the Chronicle without a definition. This section supplies one from usage only.

- **Substrate.** The mathematical-physical layer beneath the perceived world. It is what the Arcane overlay reads (mass, velocity, composition, coordinates) and what the Arcane Guru edits (time, gravity). It is also what the Nature Master perceives as life-force channels and the Necrotic Master perceives as the death boundary — the same layer read through different schools. Nothing in canon says these are different substrates.
- **Node.** A fixed geographic locus where the substrate is more responsive. Nodes are tethered to terrain features (a lake basin, a range, a reef, a desert oasis). A node's *activity level* is a measurable reading; the Firstday Watch has kept phase ledgers of it since before the Concordat, and readings have not returned to pre-1218 baseline.
- **Node activity and beasts.** Beast tier cannot exceed the local node activity level (ratified constraint). Guru-tier manifestations are lunar embodiments tethered to a node; they are displaced by unseating the node's substrate anchor, not by killing (Frostvatn, 1230–1232).
- **Node activity and casting.** Decided 11 September 2026: casting cost varies with moon phase only. Node activity governs what emerges from the substrate (beast tier, biome effects, manifestations), not what a practitioner pays to act on it. A Guru at Frostvatn paid the same for the Iselva diversion as anywhere else in Velquor; the node made the enemy, not the bill.
- **Dormant node.** A region with a node reading too low to sustain Master-tier or higher emergence.

## Magic and Martial Schools Ranks

- Novice
- Adept
- Master
- Guru

## Abilities of Magic Schools by Rank

- Elemental
    - Novice: Ignite small fires, create minor water, generate light gusts.
    - Adept: Minor elemental effects at personal scale. Control imprecise. The practitioner learns which element responds.
    - Master: Elemental production at functional scale. Sub-discipline specialization becomes pronounced.
    - Guru: Sustained works — persistent large-scale alterations (a harbor kept ice-free, an aquifer held raised) that continue drawing cost after casting ends. The body as an ongoing power source maps directly onto fatigue→organ strain→collapse, and matches the institutional note that every faction wants Elementals: a Guru is strategic infrastructure that consumes itself.
- Arcane
    - Novice: Telekinesis (light objects), force application.
    - Adept: Observation and discipline. Ambient abilities active. Reads structure before manipulating it. Training emphasizes restraint — rushing produces burnout.
    - Master: Arcane overlay — a perceptual mode rendering the world through its mathematical substrate: mass, velocity, elemental composition, coordinates, temperature, momentum. Every object becomes data. Predictions become accurate because perception becomes total.
    - Guru: Substrate editing. The overlay made writable — the Guru revises values the Master can only read. Two variables accept revision: time and gravity. Time dilation is volumetric — a bounded region runs slow or fast against its surroundings; ratio, radius, and duration are set at casting, and each multiplies cost. Gravity manipulation is directional — magnitude and vector within a bounded region: pinning a target, arresting a collapse, redirecting a fall. Edits hold only under active attention; the substrate reasserts itself the moment focus breaks. Nothing is created — existing quantities are redistributed within what the mathematics permits. Write access presumes read access: the overlay must be running to edit, so every Guru casting carries Master-rank perceptual load beneath it. A Guru inside their own dilated region desynchronizes — subjective time diverges from external time, and accumulated divergence feeds the time-lag and memory costs directly. Doctrine therefore restricts casting to exterior placement; editing a volume you occupy compounds the damage.
- Nature
    - Novice: Sense condition of nearby flora and fauna, rudimentary animal communication, summon and hold attention of small wildlife.
    - Adept: Directed attunement. Reads living systems beyond ambient sensing — not just that a tree is dying but where the damage sits. Contact deepens reading. The Novice learns to listen without intervening.
    - Master: Somatic integration. Perceives biological systems as a network of life-force channels. Through sustained contact, redirects channels to manage life force. Perception is relational, not mathematical — feels what the body needs and builds a pathway. Cannot always cure. Is there anyway.
    - Guru: Deliberate morphic borrowing — intentionally taking on animal soma (a hawk's eyes, a wolf's endurance) for bounded periods. This recontextualizes species bleed as the residue of the school's peak technique rather than an arbitrary side effect, exactly as Arcane's cost is residue of its peak technique.
- Necrotic
    - Novice: Sense proximity to death — a room where someone died feels different. A battlefield is overwhelming.
    - Adept: Death attunement. Senses the boundary being crossed — a general impression without specifics. Perceives that something died, approximately when, and the emotional register of the crossing (violent, peaceful, confused, empty).
    - Master: Boundary reading. Perceives the moment of crossing in analytic detail — the instant consciousness was and was not. Reconstructs the emotional and experiential texture of death events. Distinguishes between deaths separated by centuries.
    - Guru: Boundary holding — keeping a crossing open briefly: the dying finish a sentence, name a killer, say goodbye. Not resurrection; a delay measured in breaths. Boundary thinning becomes the occupational cost of standing in the doorway.
- Divine
    - Novice: Speech carries intent beyond natural charisma, presence produces calm in distressed individuals, touch steadies pain without treating source.
    - Adept: Directed intent. Voice and presence carry physical weight. Encouragement lands physically. Reassurance slows a racing pulse. Indistinguishable from exceptional leadership to an outside observer. Distinguishable to the recipient.
    - Master: Boon application. Positive reinforcement made physical. Imbues confidence, endurance, resolve through sustained contact or directed speech.
    - Guru: Lifespan transference — explicit transfer of years from self to recipient. This converts the cost mechanic from consequence into the ability itself: the Guru doesn't suffer compression as a side effect, compression is the spell. Strongest thematic fit with the Emotional Payload text you already have ("each healing is a subtraction").

## Magic School Cost

- Elemental
    **Cost progression:** Fatigue → muscle failure → organ strain → collapse.

    **Institutional role:** Pervades infrastructure — military engineering, agriculture, environmental management. Most visually dramatic and most easily weaponized. Every faction wants them. Every faction fears the other faction having them.

    **Emotional Payload:** The muscle failure is not just weakness — it's the specific humiliation of needing help to stand, of watching your hands shake when you try to hold a cup, of knowing that every deployment costs something your body cannot replenish. You were the strongest person you knew. Now you can't open a jar. You pretend it's not happening. Your partner pretends not to notice. The organ strain is the moment you feel your heart skip and realize this might be the last time — the last time you hold them, the last time you see the sun, the last time you feel the fire answer your call. The collapse is not just death — it's the final failure of a body you pushed too hard, too long, for causes that seemed important at the time. Was it worth it? You'll never know. You're already gone.
- Arcane
    **Cost progression:** Headache → tremor → time lag perception → memory dissonance → identity-time fracture.

    **Institutional role:** Dominates the Rationalist institution's research and documentation apparatus. The institution that determines truth is staffed by people who perceive truth as mathematical structure. What cannot be measured tends to be classified as anecdotal. This creates a structural bias the institution cannot self-diagnose.

    **Emotional Payload:** The memory dissonance is not just confusion — it's the specific terror of not trusting your own mind. The moment you realize you can't remember whether you locked the door, whether you said goodbye, whether the person standing in front of you is someone you love or someone you barely know. You pause mid-sentence, waiting for the white sparks to clear, and you can't remember what you were going to say. Your child looks at you with concern they're too young to hide. You smile. You pretend. But you know — something is eroding. The identity-time fracture is the ultimate loneliness — losing the thread of who you are, watching yourself from outside, knowing the person everyone loves is already gone and you're just the memory of them, still walking around, still pretending.
- Nature
    **Cost progression:** Scar tissue → sensory narrowing → partial morphic blending → species bleed.

    **Institutional role:** Operates outside the Rationalist treatment framework. The Rationalist institution classifies Nature methods as "experimental mercy techniques" — acknowledging efficacy while denying legitimacy. The institution cannot heal, so it studies the healer until healing becomes a procedure that scales. This is the narrative's central institutional critique.

    **Emotional Payload:** Sensory narrowing is not just reduced sensation — it's the specific loss of a sense you didn't appreciate until it was gone. The smell of rain. The texture of a child's hair. The warmth of sunlight on skin. You remember having them. You remember taking them for granted. Now you press your face into your lover's neck and feel nothing. You smile anyway. You pretend. Species bleed is the horror of becoming something other, of feeling the animal rise in you, of no longer knowing whether you're human or something else. You catch yourself sniffing the air. You catch yourself wanting to run on all fours. You catch yourself looking at your hands and not recognizing them as yours.
- Necrotic
    **Cost progression:** Skin pallor → death residue sensation → touch erosion → boundary thinning.

    **Physical practice:** Necrotic practitioners work bare-handed — gloves muffle attunement. Phantom sensations map onto the living body. Self-grounding through controlled pain maintains the boundary between self and the dead.

    **Institutional role [N]:** Ethosless by charter vacancy. Purged from Crown territory 1164–1166; registered but distrusted in Accord territory, where a boundary reading is inadmissible as evidence under the standing doctrine that what cannot be measured is anecdotal (Baishui, 1211); received in Chimera territory, which took the Purge refugees and does not register anyone. The one school whose peak testimony neither certifying institution will admit, and the only one that has twice named a killer.

    **Emotional Payload:** Touch erosion is not just numbness — it's the specific loss of connection to the living. The moment you realize you can't feel your lover's hand anymore, not really — that there's always something between you, something cold, something that belongs to the dead. You hold them anyway. You pretend. They pretend not to notice. But you know — you're already half-gone. The boundary thinning is the terror of not knowing where you end and they begin. You wake at 3:47, the average time of death in your region, and you don't know if the cold beside you is your partner or something else. You don't know if it matters anymore.
- Divine
    **Cost progression:** Minor aging → frailty → organ degradation → lifespan compression.

    **Institutional role [N]:** The Crown's sacramental school. Attunement is verified by the Indigo Assay; Apostle certification is benefice-bearing, and fraud voids benefice. In Accord territory Divine practitioners register like any other school, and the Ledger records their cost as aging in years — the same figure the Crown records as sacrament and the Ledger as depreciation. Under Crown doctrine (Part VII) a Divine healing is a ledgered transfer of lent years, which is why a Rationalist examining body holds Crown charter: the Crown needs counters.

    **Emotional Payload:** The aging is not just wrinkles — it's watching your children grow up while you grow old too fast. It's your spouse's hand in yours, their skin still young, yours already papery. It's knowing you will leave them sooner than you should, and every healing you do makes it sooner. The frailty is the moment you can't keep up with your own child, the moment they have to wait for you, the moment they realize you won't be there for their graduation, their wedding, their children. The organ degradation is the specific pain of knowing your body is failing and choosing to use it anyway, to heal one more person, to give one more day to someone else's loved one, even as it costs you a day of your own. The lifespan compression is the arithmetic of grief — each healing is a subtraction, each saved life is a loss, and you do it anyway because the alternative is letting them die, and you cannot let them die, even if it means dying sooner yourself.

# Part III — Martial Schools [C]

Ratified text from Canon.md. The Ranger pairing uses a dagger, which is not a ranked martial school; the Longbow Novice entry covers it ("the dagger covers the bow's blind distance") and no separate progression exists. Not a gap — a design decision — recorded here so it is not re-raised.

## Martial Schools

- Sword/Shield
- Glaive
- Longbow
- Hammer
- Ax

## Martial Schools Rank abilities
> Martial abilities work different from magic. Each class is proficient in their Martial school, even at novice. Novice -> Guru progresses exponentially. A Guru in their Martial School can fight platoons of foot soldiers at once.

Martial cost follows the same law as magic cost — severity nearly equal to power — but the ceiling is lower (a platoon, not a harbor), so the price is survivable and total: the discipline consumes the body and the life rather than the lifespan. A martial Novice is already a competent soldier; a magic Novice is barely functional. The curves cross at Master.

- Sword/Shield
    - Novice: Line-competent. Shield-wall discipline, cuts and covers drilled to reflex. Guards a door, holds a place in a wall, keeps a principal alive in a crowd.
    - Adept: The shield becomes the second weapon — binds, checks, blind-angle control. Reads an opponent's weight before the commitment. Duels above their number.
    - Master: Complete defense, and the counter-practitioner craft: reads casting posture and breath the way others read a sword arm, takes flasks and bolts on the boss or not at all, closes the distance inside a chant. The escort of record against practitioners.
    - Guru: The fight is spent before contact — positioning so exact that a formation's options retire one by one. Fights a platoon by dismantling its cohesion: kills the corners, folds the line, turns numbers into a queue. Calibration: the platoon standard expressed as attrition of structure, not of men first.
- Glaive
    - Novice: Formation reach fighter. Keeps spacing, sets the point, competent in rank-and-file polearm drill.
    - Adept: Zone control at personal scale — the two-pace circle nobody enters standing. Fluid transitions between blade, haft, and butt.
    - Master: Battlefield geometry. Reads the flow of a melee the way doctrine reads a map; anchors a line's hinge; denies a corridor alone.
    - Guru: Moving area denial. The circle is wherever the Guru walks; a platoon engaging one is not outnumbered so much as unscheduled — it cannot bring numbers to bear faster than the reach retires them. Steel Quorum doctrine prices a Guru Vanguard as terrain. Calibration: the platoon standard expressed as space.
- Longbow
    - Novice: Volley-competent archer and camp scout. Keeps a watch, reads ground. The dagger covers the bow's blind distance (the Ranger pairing).
    - Adept: Marks moving targets at range, tracks across biomes, shoots from concealment without surrendering it.
    - Master: Overwatch. A slope, a treeline, a pass held by one bow — shots arrive where the target will be, not where it is. Master overwatch is what made the Mwamba foothills impassable.
    - Guru: Denial at landscape scale. A Guru on prepared ground stops a platoon's advance not by killing fifty but by making the first ten deaths unanswerable — no seen shooter, no counted arrows, no safe pace. Fear does the arithmetic. Calibration: the platoon standard expressed as ground the platoon will not cross.
- Hammer
    - Novice: Breach-line infantry. Shields split, doors opened, footing kept under weight.
    - Adept: Structural hitting — strikes the join, not the mass. Armor and barricade read as seams.
    - Master: The charge-stopper. Sets against cavalry or a rush and the rush ends there; breaches a barred gate by reading its load path. Why the columns held anything flat.
    - Guru: Formation-breaking as demolition. A Guru Paladin hits a shield wall where its weight is stored and the wall fails as a structure, not as men. A platoon fights what feels like a collapsing building that walks. Calibration: the platoon standard expressed as breakage.
- Ax
    - Novice: Shock infantry. Commits harder than trained fear allows; first over the barricade.
    - Adept: The gate — trained suppression of pain and fear for the length of an assault. Hits that end exchanges.
    - Master: Sustained frenzy under direction. A Master berserker is aimable, holds the gate open through a full engagement, and is worth a breach alone.
    - Guru: The gate opens all the way. A Guru in frenzy does not register wounds, numbers, or time; the engagement ends when nothing in reach stands, and the companions' first duty is the calling-back. Calibration: the platoon standard expressed literally, and priced in the cost table.

## Martial School Cost

- Sword/Shield
    **Cost progression:** Scar accumulation → residue mapping → startle lock → the guard that will not drop.

    **Institutional role:** The Ethosless martial school of record. The counter-practitioner escort trade runs on hexblades; so do the Unsworn's line contracts and the Off-Ledger's protection work. The Purge scattered Crown-territory hexblades into exactly the compacts the Crown now hires to guard its envoys — a fact every party involved invoices around and no party mentions.

    **Emotional Payload:** The residue mapping is not just scarring — it's your body becoming a record of every school that ever touched it. The patch on your forearm that stays cold in summer. The burn that aches when the red moon is up. You know which scar is which; you were there for all of them. The startle lock is the guard reflex colonizing your life — the shield arm that rises when your child runs at you, the chair you cannot take unless it faces the door. And the guard that will not drop is the day you realize no one has touched you unexpectedly in years, because you have, carefully, methodically, made unexpected touch impossible — and the people who love you learned the rule so long ago they no longer remember learning it.
- Glaive
    **Cost progression:** Joint wear → conditioning debt → spinal compression → the seizing.

    **Institutional role:** The Rationalist teaching school. Vanguard doctrine is written, examined, and certified; it is the Accord's garrison standard, and the Steel Quorum sells it by the volume to anyone with the fee.

    **Emotional Payload:** The conditioning debt is not just training — it's two hours every morning, forever, because the art is a wound-up mechanism and the day you skip is the day the shoulders begin to lock. You measure rooms by reach without meaning to. Your partner learned your circle years ago, learned to enter it announced, and you never asked them to and they never said they had. The seizing comes for the spine last — you stand like a bent pike, and the young ones pretend the stoop is dignity, and you let them, because the alternative is explaining that the discipline that made you unpassable has finished passing through you.
- Longbow
    **Cost progression:** Draw-side deformation → watch-broken sleep → sensory tuning → the unclosing eye.

    **Institutional role:** The Reverie frontier school and Ash-and-Antler's working art. Warding contracts price Master overwatch by the season; the Accord licenses what it cannot staff.

    **Emotional Payload:** The deformation is not just posture — it's the right shoulder that sits higher, the spine curved to the draw, the tailor who adjusts for it without asking because everyone in this town knows what you are. The watch-broken sleep is the sentry the discipline built inside you, still on duty decades after the last contract — you have not slept through a night since training and you no longer call it a problem, which is the problem. The tuning cannot be turned down: you hear the difference between wind in leaves and wind over drawn fletching at two hundred paces, and at your grandchild's naming-day you counted the exits, and caught yourself, and counted them again anyway. The eye that never closes was the price of every pass you held. The passes held.
- Hammer
    **Cost progression:** Micro-fracture conditioning → bone remodeling → deep-set brittleness → the settling.

    **Institutional role:** The Crown's arm. Paladin columns escort the Namaris rites and answer the Chancel's problems that have walls. A century of record says they hold anything flat; the same record is quieter about trees.

    **Emotional Payload:** The conditioning is not just toughness — it's bones densified through years of controlled breaking, a skeleton any Assay reading shows as decades of healed fractures, laid down deliberately, like courses of masonry. Strong young. Brittle early. The settling is the bill arriving at fifty: the cough that cracks a rib, the pew you brace on rising, the winter stairs taken like a negotiation. You stopped a cavalry charge once — planted, set, and the horses broke around you like water. Now the young paladins carry your maul to the wall for you, as an honor. It is an honor. It is also that you cannot.
- Ax
    **Cost progression:** Post-gate collapse → frenzy amnesia → affect blunting → heart-burn.

    **Institutional role:** The Reverie shock school and the core of the tribal musters. The Antler War taught the Crown's columns the difference between holding flat ground and entering trees; the lesson was mostly delivered with axes.

    **Emotional Payload:** The amnesia is not just forgetting — it's that you were never there. Others tell you what you did, choosing their words with a care you have learned to read, and you carry secondhand memories of your own hands. The blunting is the gate failing to close cleanly: each opening holds back the fear, and each year it holds back a little of everything else — joy arrives muffled, grief arrives late, your child's laughter reaches you like sound through water, and you laugh anyway, on time, from practice. And the heart, which you spent like coin every time the gate opened, keeps its own count. Berserkers' companions train for the calling-back. Every berserker knows there is a day the call goes unanswered, and does the work anyway, because the work was never for them.

# Part IV — Alchemy [C]

Ratified text from Canon.md.

## Alchemy
> The use of potions, flasks, poisons, familiars, golems and gadgets. Alchemy is split into two arts. **Light Arts** — flasks, familiars, and gadgets: the Artificer's craft, Ledger-certified like a casting license. **Dark Arts** — poisons, potions, and golems: the Warlock's craft, certified by no one and purchased by everyone.

## Alchemy Rank abilities

- Light Arts (Artificer)
    - Novice: Field kit competence. Stable flasks (fire, smoke, adhesive), simple gadgets, maintenance of other hands' work.
    - Adept: Bench craft. Bespoke flasks with timed or conditional triggers; a first familiar — a small bound construct on a wren, rat, or beetle frame that carries, watches, fetches. The bond is the craft.
    - Master: Workshop scale. Familiars with specialized senses working at a distance; gadget systems — locks, lifts, signal lines; production discipline others can be trained on. The Accord's arsenals run on Master artificers.
    - Guru: Integration. A working household of familiars acting in concert — eyes, hands, and tools distributed across a battlefield or a city ward. The platoon standard expressed as logistics: one Guru equips, informs, and repairs a company from a wagon.
- Dark Arts (Warlock)
    - Novice: Handles what others must not. Stable poisons, purgatives, preservation draughts.
    - Adept: Graded venoms with chosen onsets; potions that push a body past its budget and present the bill later; a first golem — vat-grown or clay-bound, strong and stupid.
    - Master: Contact-grade work — a Master's poison needs skin, not a cup (the Baishui wax was Master work at minimum). Potions tailored to a named recipient. Golems that hold simple standing orders.
    - Guru: The pharmacopoeia entire. Dosing that steers crowds — wells, granaries, censers — the platoon standard by other means; golem teams under standing orders; antidote craft no one else can check. The Purge made Gurus of this art rare, unregistered, and priced accordingly.

## Alchemy Cost

- Light Arts
    **Cost progression:** Reagent-etched hands → close-work sight → fume-worn lungs → the divided attention.

    **Institutional role:** The Rationalist workshop apparatus. Artificer certification is Ledger-registered like a casting license. Familiars are property in Accord law and something less simple in Chimera territory, where a bound construct that watches and fetches sits uncomfortably close to things the Sedge Circles have opinions about.

    **Emotional Payload:** The hands are not just worn — they're a map of every mistake you survived learning from: the acid-white fingertips, the burn like spilled wax, the two numb knuckles from the winter you rushed. The sight is the bench claiming your eyes — perfect at eight inches, and you squint at your daughter across the dinner table and she has stopped mentioning it. The divided attention is the familiars' true price: each bond is a thread of your focus that never comes back, and your family has learned the particular quality of your presence, seven-eighths there. The wren on the windowsill attends your son's recital because you, in some divided way, are watching through it. Your son has learned to bow to the bird. You have not decided how you feel about that, with the eighth of you that decides.
- Dark Arts
    **Cost progression:** Tolerance dosing → organ staining → taste-death → the venomous body.

    **Institutional role:** Ethosless by charter vacancy — no institution licenses the Dark Arts and every institution buys them. The Second Register's poison-precursor controls exist because of this art. Purge-scattered, Meridian-moved, priced accordingly.

    **Emotional Payload:** The dosing is not just discipline — it's poisoning yourself every morning, measured, on schedule, so that nothing can ever poison you again, and being right. Food went first: bread like paper, fruit like water, your mother's cooking a memory you keep by memory alone. Then the staining — the nailbeds, the whites of the eyes, the tint that makes healers hesitate, and you learn to warn them before they touch you, and to watch them decide whether to. The venomous body is the last ledger entry: you will die immune to everything except what the immunities cost, your blood a hazard, your kiss a risk you disclose, and whoever prepares your body must be paid triple and told why. You wrote that instruction yourself, years ago. You update the fee for inflation.

**Why is Baishui rich?** It sells certification. Every practitioner, miller, pilot, and midwife on the Accord's side of the map pays a board fee at Baishui or to an examiner it trained, the fee scales with standing, and the seal is worth nothing unless Baishui issues it. **What does an ordinary Isavík household spend most of its income on?** Grain it did not grow. Fish is what it has; bread is what it buys. Second is the parish assessment, third is iron. ## Culture Profiles [C]

From the Culture addendum; ratified 11 September 2026 (R.0 #4). Structural note first, then one profile per culture.

**Structural note.** The alignment produces an asymmetry the cultures live inside. Two cultures sit whole within a single charter: the Renlei entirely under the Blue Accord, the Tangata entirely under the Green Chimera. The other four are split — Menneske across Accord and Crown, Unsaan across Crown and Accord, Yamana across Crown and Chimera, Umutu across Crown and Chimera. The whole-charter cultures tend to confuse their customs with their institution's procedures, and to be blamed by outsiders for their institution's policies. The split cultures carry the century's conflicts internally: the 1172 fleet actions were Menneske firing on Menneske, the Purge was administered by Unsaan from an Unsaan citadel, the Edict of Amilpan was issued from a Yamana town against practices Yamana kin hold sacred, and the Antler War burned across Umutu ground from both directions. Culture is not institution. The four split cultures are the proof, and they know it.

## Menneske

**Homelands:** Isavík (Gold Crown) and Grimskov (Blue Accord), facing each other across Svarthavet. **Biomes:** tundra, taiga, evergreen, grassland. **Inspiration register:** Scandinavian.

One language, low dialect drift — the strait keeps both shores talking, trading, and marrying. The written register is sparse by design: Menneske writing exists to remind, not to bind. What binds is the spoken word.

**Governance and law.** Beneath both charters, the moot persists — the standing assembly of a settlement's households, convened on fixed days. In Grimskov, Accord examiners hold the offices but must present rulings to the moot for *hearing*. Not approval; hearing. The distinction is load-bearing to Menneske and invisible to Accord auditors, who have filed a century of reports noting that the assemblies "serve no procedural function" and continue to convene. In Isavík, Crown parishes schedule the Namaris rites around moot days rather than over them, a courtesy the Chancel's manuals do not mention and its local sacrists do not skip.

Three laws travel with every Menneske household. **Witness-law:** a thing said before three witnesses binds; paper is the memory of the word, not the word itself. Menneske comply fully with the Manifest Rule and resent it fully, as a demand to say everything twice. **Ice-law:** the first keel through new harbor ice held that channel's toll for the winter — a custom five centuries old, suspended at Stenhavn from 1169 to 1174 because no keel got through, and quietly extinguished there by the Escrow Convention's lease terms. A small grief folded inside the larger one; Stenhavn pilots still name the channel lines after the last families to hold them. **Winter-count:** sentences, exiles, and debts of honor are measured in winters, not years. The Ledger records "three winters" as three years with an explanatory note. The note is wrong in ways no Menneske has bothered to correct.

**Ritual.** The Firstday Watch keeps 1 Velquor everywhere, but the Menneske keep the eve: the Longest Night held awake, households lamp-lit until dawn, the year's debts spoken aloud and either forgiven or affirmed before the calendar turns. Since 1230 the observance carries a second weight that nobody names — the Frostvatn thing rose on that night, and every Menneske knows it kept the old appointment.

**Daily life.** Timber-and-turf longhouses banked against wind; fish, barley, and imported grain — the Frost taught the whole culture exactly how much imported. Timber comes down from Ulvskov, wool and horses from the Kaldmark grasslands, everything else across water. Sledges and shag ponies inland, and boats before almost anything else: a Menneske child can usually row before reliably lying.

**Folklore.** Every deep lake has a mother. The Vatnmor counts the steps of children who skate on thin ice and keeps the ones who miscount — a tale for producing careful children, told the same way for generations. In Hvítadal the tellers now end it differently: *she counted, and we left.* The Ledger classifies the older Vatnmor material as anecdotal. Menneske consider that the Ledger's problem, and after 1230, demonstrably so.

**Frictions.** With the Renlei: paper against word. The arbitration after the 1172 fleet actions failed in part because Renlei arbiters weighted manifests over sworn testimony; Menneske heard themselves being called liars, politely, in writing. With the Unsaan: silence. Menneske silence is consideration, and assent can live inside it; Unsaan negotiation reads a long pause as refusal or offense, and fills it. Bahr Sufra grain contracts have misfired on this for a century — each side courteous, each side certain the other just withdrew. Internally: cross-strait marriages file twice, once with a Crown parish and once with an Accord registry, and every Menneske wedding toast includes a joke about which copy is real. The answer, per witness-law, is neither.

## Renlei

**Homelands:** Zhenshui and Tienling, both Blue Accord. **Biomes:** taiga, evergreen, grassland, desert edge. **Inspiration register:** East Asian.

The one culture wholly inside a single charter, with the consequences the structural note predicts. Renlei often cannot say where custom ends and Accord procedure begins, because for six generations they have been the same offices. And outsiders blame Renlei for Accord policy: the customs cutters at Shakoui flew Accord colors, but the guild that buried nine crew remembers the officers' names, and the names were Renlei.

**Governance and law.** Examination boards all the way down. Civil standing accrues by certification, and not only for practitioners — millers, pilots, midwives, and well-diggers sit for boards, and a village posts its certificate wall where another culture would post its shrine. Three laws define the register. **Seal law:** authority lives in the seal, not the signer. A sealed unsigned document binds; a signed unsealed one is a draft. Seal forgery was made a capital matter during the Manifest War and has stayed one. **Phase-contract law:** every contract names the moon phase of performance, because cost does — "an Orrivane price" is cheap and "a Velquor price" is not. The phase-pegged tariffs of the 1205 Ledger Accords were not an innovation; they were Renlei commercial practice nationalized. **Examination standing:** disputes between parties of unequal certification are heard by an examiner of higher standing than either, which in remote districts can mean waiting a season for one to arrive. Renlei wait.

**Ritual.** Board days are civic festivals — a village whose candidate passes feeds everyone, including the examiner, who by regulation pays for his own portion and by custom is never allowed to. The five phase-turn days are kept as market truces and family observances; the Velquor turn is for sweeping graves and settling the year's sealed accounts.

**Daily life.** River and canal life along the Lianshui: barge trains, ox roads, portal stations at the seats. Millet and river fish in the north, dryland wheat toward the desert edge. The caravan towns are their own register — Shakou and Yunmen remember the Interdiction as *the fat years*, three years when every portal stood dark and everything moved by road through their gates. A town that profited from disaster, knows it, and holds an annual lantern feast it does not explain to visitors.

**Folklore.** Since the Lag, the Ninth-Day Traveler: a figure who knocks at dusk asking for news that is nine days stale. The custom is exact — feed him, answer as if the news were new, and never tell him the date. Houses near Yunmen keep a ninth-day bowl by the door. The folklore is honest about its origin; the dilation fault produced real travelers out of true, and some of them were a long time finding their way back to the calendar. The tale is the culture agreeing to be kind about it forever.

**Frictions.** With the Menneske, reciprocally: Renlei find oath-only dealing unauditable — a word before three witnesses is, in the standard joke, three more people to bribe. With the Tangata: Puketai. Renlei captains observe the Passage Agreements to the letter while their own institution maintains the Agreements cannot exist. Renlei hold both positions without discomfort, which the Tangata find funnier than offensive. With the Umutu: at Tinwara and Marendi, truce ground means no claims pressed — and a Renlei factor who presses a sealed debt at market has committed a serious offense while following his own law precisely. Two cultures, two meanings of what may not be drawn on truce ground: the Umutu mean papers as much as knives.

## Unsaan

**Homelands:** Bahr-Al-Asra (Gold Crown) and Zand-Kala (Blue Accord). **Biomes:** savanna, desert, grassland, deciduous forest. **Inspiration register:** Middle Eastern, in two branches.

One language, two registers, and the split is audible in the map itself: Asran, the register of the citadel and the rites (Marj-Al-Sidr, Qasr-Al-Rimal, Bab-Al-Ghaf), and Kalani, the register of gardens and contracts (Dasht-Anar, Sabz-Rud, Bagh-Shirin). Asran is what one prays and rules in; Kalani is what one plants and bargains in. Most Unsaan hold both and switch mid-sentence; which register a speaker chooses for a given clause is itself information.

**Governance and law.** Bahr-Al-Asra is governed through arbiter-judges seated under the Chancel; Zand-Kala through garden councils whose seats are filled by Accord examination — the current First Examiner, Golnar Sabz, came up through them. Three laws are held in common across the split. **Guest-right:** three nights inviolable; the host answers for the guest's conduct; and not until the third night may the host ask the guest's business. **Shade-and-water servitudes:** a wall's midday shade belongs to the road, not the wall's owner, and town wells carry public draw-hours. **Name law:** an office-holder takes the name of the place that holds the office, so that the office cannot pass by blood — the name dies back to the place at each succession. The Crown's al-Rimal convention is this law, formalized: every Sacrist-General takes the citadel's name and surrenders their own, which is why a Menneske-born Sunniva and a Teocalli-born Citlali could hold an Unsaan citadel's seat without an Unsaan dynasty ever forming. The law's purpose is exactly that.

**The Purge, at home.** The Purge of 1164–1166 was administered from Qasr-Al-Rimal, and it collided with guest-right in every Asran town it touched. Households that held to the three nights sheltered the purged and answered for it; both registers still cite, carefully, *the third-night questions of 1164–66* — the years when asking a guest's business became an act of the state, and the law's protection became its trap. Zand-Kala, across the charter line, absorbed families who converted their names to garden-names and their pasts to blank pages. Kalani has a particular way of not asking about a garden-name that is itself a form of courtesy.

**Ritual.** The Namaris rites at the citadel are the Crown's. The observance the whole culture shares is older: the sitting of accounts at each phase turn, when a household's ledger is read aloud — and the ledger tallies debts of hospitality, not money. Who was fed, who was sheltered, who owes a roof. An Unsaan household's standing is this ledger, and everyone present knows it by heart before it is read.

**Daily life.** Camel and horse strings on the Bahr Sufra; fig and date orchards on the savanna meadows; the forest gate at Bab-Al-Ghaf taxing the hardwood trade. Zand-Kala is channeled irrigation and pomegranate gardens, courtyard houses built around water, and the best contract lawyers on either continent, a fact the rest of Terhia states as complaint and Zand-Kala accepts as review.

**Folklore.** The Unwritten Guest: a guest arrives whose name cannot be said or written, and the household keeps the three nights anyway. The tale ends differently by register. In Asran, the fourth morning finds the guest gone and the household's dead prayed for in a hand no one knows. In Kalani, the fourth morning finds the household's page in the tax register blank — and it stays blank, and no one ever comes to collect. Both endings are about the same thing: what sheltering the nameless costs, and what it pays. No teller mentions the Purge. No listener needs it mentioned.

**Frictions.** With the Menneske, reciprocally: Unsaan fill silence as courtesy — to leave a guest sitting in silence is to starve him at the table — and Menneske read the filling as pressure. With the Umutu: well law against well law. An Unsaan town well is a servitude with draw-hours; an Umutu well is a trust with give-back debts. An Umutu drover working the windlass to *pay the well back* at an Unsaan public well reads, locally, as a stranger fouling the mechanism — a recorded cause of brawls at the Sufra crossings for as long as there have been records. Internally: the citadel register hears Kalani contract-speech as haggling in church; Kalani hears Asran rite-speech as a price hidden inside a prayer. Both are describing the same national talent from opposite ends.

## Yamana

**Homelands:** Teocalli (Gold Crown) and Yaxkulu (Green Chimera), adjacent on the western continent. **Biomes:** deciduous forest, grassland, chaparral, desert edge. **Inspiration register:** Mesoamerican.

The culture the century split deepest. Teocalli holds the Crown's ritual heartland; Yaxkulu is tribal territory seated in the Bramble Concord; Ash-and-Antler's Yamana founding lineages are Yaxkulu. The Edict of Amilpan — reverie proscribed throughout Crown territory — was issued from a Yamana irrigation town, and its nearest targets were Yamana kin across the border. During the Antler War the two homelands never fought, which is not the same as peace: border crossings closed, the Sakbeha causeway checkpoints doubled, and families learned which cousins could no longer be written to. The Yamana call those years *the war we did not quite fight*, and mean the phrase precisely.

**Governance and law.** Teocalli is administered through the temple precincts under the Chancel; Yaxkulu through speaker-circles seated in the Concord. What both run on is the roads. **Road law:** the white causeways — raised, paved, dead straight between settlements and precincts — are right-of-way and sanctuary both. Blood spilled on a causeway owes double penance, a rule older than the Crown's presence. The Tepemitla assassination was therefore, to every Yamana, a double sacrilege before the theologians finished defining the first one: not merely a Sacrist-General killed, but killed *on the road*. **Duty inheritance:** obligations survive the dead. An unmet duty passes with the estate, and can be older than anyone living — road-keeping duties usually are. A Yamana introduces themselves, formally, by their oldest inherited duty. **Water law at the terraces:** the same irrigation customs run in both homelands under different paperwork — Amilpan's turns are allotted on the Namaris circuit calendar under the Crown, Yaxkulu's by Threnis claim through the Concord. Same water, same customs, two charters. The farmers find this funnier than either charter does.

**Ritual.** Road-sweeping processions at every phase turn, in both homelands, under both charters — the one shared rite the Edict could not touch, because the Crown's own processions use the causeways. A grandmother in Xalcoyan and her proscribed sister in Kinha sweep the same road from opposite ends. The Edict's enforcement officers know this. There is no record of one interfering.

**Daily life.** Hill towns and terraces in the deciduous forest; maize on the terrace walls, beans and chiles in the margins; chaparral scrub grazing at Xalcoyan; the desert wells at Kinha. No heavy draft tradition — the causeways made one unnecessary. Instead, porters, pack llamas on the hill routes, and the runner lineages: causeway runners carry news faster than riders, hold formal standing in both homelands, and are the one profession the border checkpoints never delayed, even in the war years. Both sides needed the news more than the advantage.

**Folklore.** The Road That Walks: somewhere in the western forest a causeway moves one stone each year toward a destination no one remembers, and every road-keeper lineage claims a stretch of it; when it arrives, the duty ends. Teocalli tellers say it walks toward a temple not yet built. Yaxkulu tellers say it walks away from one already abandoned. It is the same road. Both tellers know that too.

**Frictions.** With the Renlei: the accounts of the dead. The Ledger closes a practitioner's file at death; Yamana probate expects the duties to transfer with the estate. A registry clerk marking a file *closed* has, more than once, informed a grieving heir that their inheritance does not exist — grief-cases that recur at registry desks wherever the two customs meet. With the Umutu: where the white roads cross the drove-lines, road law says the road is sanctuary and passage law says the herd does not stop. The compromise, everywhere, is culverts and ramps, and the argument about who maintains them, held annually, is by now functionally a shared holiday. Internally: the Edict. A practice that is a crime on one side of a causeway and an inheritance on the other, sometimes within a single family — the split the culture manages daily, quietly, and has never once brought to either charter for resolution, on the shared understanding that neither charter would survive being asked.

## Umutu

**Homelands:** Oasira (Gold Crown) and Zulwazi (Green Chimera). **Biomes:** savanna, desert, grassland, chaparral. **Inspiration register:** Saharan African.

The Antler War was fought across Umutu ground from both directions, and the peace was signed on it: Tinwara, where the treaty was made, is Umutu market-truce ground — the only ground both belligerents could stand on, which is why the negotiators stood on it.

**Governance and law.** Beneath the Crown's shrine parishes in Oasira and the Concord claims of Zulwazi, the culture runs on well-keeper lineages and age-set councils. **Water law:** wells are trusts, never property. Every draw creates a give-back debt — labor on the well, not water — and the well's ledger is kept by its keeper lineage. *The well remembers* is a legal maxim first and folklore second. **Passage law:** the drove-lines have precedence over planted verge in drought years, and the herds do not stop. The shrine burnings of 1178–80 carry a legal texture the Crown's account omits: the new Namaris shrines were built on drove-lines. To the Crown, shrines; to Umutu herders, enclosure of the road. In Umutu law the war's first fires were the removal of an obstruction — which does not change what followed, and does change what it was. **Market truce:** on truce ground no claims are pressed, no instruments drawn — knives or papers — and all parties feed at one fire. The record credits the Marendi Standstill of 1184 to Unsworn compact rules. Marendi credits the ground. Both explanations are read aloud, in that order, at the annual truce renewal, and no one has ever proposed choosing between them.

**Ritual.** Well ceremonies in the Saelura phase, when the water holds — deepening, lining, and give-back labor scheduled to the red moon, because that is when the desert's oases stabilize and the work keeps. Age-sets are cut by birth phase: five cohorts a year, each named, each initiated together, each carrying its phase-name for life. Ask an Umutu their age and the answer is a cohort, not a number.

**The Benefice Crisis, at home.** Oasira bore it. The voided miracles were Oasira healings; parishes that had tithed against Chancel benefices lost twice, once in the tithe and once in the paper. The post-crisis land sales put some well ground into Accord-creditor hands — a third claimant standing between parish and keeper lineage that neither of them wanted and both of them, for once, agreed about. The Zulwazi speakers declined to gloat in the Concord minutes. The minutes record the declining.

**Daily life.** Cattle on the savanna, the seasonal circuits of the drove-lines, mud-brick and thorn-fence compounds, the well town at Azelra, chaparral highlands at Kwazira, river trade down the Mto Azari to Marendi's grassland market. Wealth is counted in cattle and standing in the well-ledgers, and a family rich in one and poor in the other knows exactly which poverty its neighbors weigh heavier.

**Folklore.** The Well That Counts: a well that yields exactly the water its drawer gave back the year before. A stranger draws and gets dust. The well-keeper draws for him and goes thirsty herself. The next year the stranger returns and works the windlass for a season, unasked. The tale is water law taught as narrative, and every Umutu child can recite it before they can be trusted with a bucket. A second tale, told smaller: Ziwa Nari is a well whose keeper never came back. Children are not told what that means. They work it out.

**Frictions.** With the Unsaan, reciprocally: Umutu read draw-hours as generosity rationed by clock — a well with office hours — and Unsaan read give-back demands on travelers as a toll dressed as virtue. With the Tangata: fire. The Rangitahu burn schedules drift smoke across Zulwazi grazing, and an unscheduled fire reads to a herder as an attack on pasture. It is the Concord docket's perennial item, renewed as reliably as the truce at Marendi. With the Crown, internal to Oasira: parish against keeper lineage over whether a shrine's well is Chancel property or a trust the Chancel happens to pray beside. The Benefice Crisis settled nothing here except that there is now a third wrong answer.

## Tangata

**Homelands:** Awanui and Koremu, both Green Chimera. **Biomes:** tropical rainforest, evergreen, grassland, red-sand desert edge. **Inspiration register:** Polynesian.

Wholly Chimera, as the Renlei are wholly Accord — the symmetric pole. The consequence runs the same direction: Tangata treat institutional charters approximately the way Renlei treat weather, as a real thing one plans around without mistaking it for law.

**Governance and law.** House-lineage councils in the settlements; navigator standing on the water — a navigator's word on conditions binds the way a Renlei seal binds paper, and for the same reason: the whole system fails if it can be doubted. Tangata claimants hold many of the Bramble Concord's Threnis-territory seats. Three bodies of law. **Way law:** channels, passes, and paths are named, have keepers, and transit is negotiated. The Puketai Passage Agreements of 1224 are not, to Tangata, a novelty — they are ordinary way law extended to a reef that started answering. Outsiders asked how one treats with coral. Tangata asked how everyone else had been failing to. **Closed ground:** a keeper or council may close ground or water by declaration; violation is a Concord matter; closures lapse at the phase turn unless renewed, which keeps the map of the forbidden current and keeps keepers honest about renewing only what they mean. **Fire law:** the Rangitahu burn rights are held by lineage, scheduled to the Threnis phase, never sold and often lent. The taxing of through-traffic on the inland routes — the practice the Ledger's files describe as the tribes taxing what they do not report — is, in Tangata law, not an irregularity but the point. The tax asserts jurisdiction over the way. A report would concede the Ledger's right to know what moves on it. The Black Meridian understands this distinction perfectly, and pays.

**Ritual.** Launchings and first-crossings mark every stage of a life. Each Tangata carries a birth-moon: for the Koremu-born it is read in Lake Marama's night water, for the Awanui-born in the swells of Te Moana Rangi, and the moon of one's birth-night is a named affiliation carried for life — not a destiny, a berth. The Passage Agreements are renewed at Puketai each Threnis phase, and the renewal has already become the harbor's chief festival, fifteen years old and conducted as if ancient, which by Tangata reckoning it is: the law it extends always was.

**Daily life.** Outriggers and river craft; the river-island seat at Waimotu; taro and fish in the rainforest settlements; fire-managed hunting country at Rangitahu; the odd branch at Onekura working salt pans on the red-sand edge and trading across to the interior. A Tangata settlement is oriented to its water the way a Renlei village is oriented to its certificate wall — the first thing shown to a guest, the last thing named in the evening.

**Folklore.** Tangata distinguish poorly, on principle, between chart and story. A named channel's tale is its sailing directions: the story of how the channel got its name encodes its hazards, and a navigator who cannot tell the story cannot claim the channel. The Ledger classifies this corpus as anecdote. Tangata note, without heat, that their anecdotes have a better wreck record than the Ledger's charts. One tale with teeth: the Keeper of the Ninth Channel — off Puketai there are eight named ways, and a ninth that exists only under Velquor, when the tides run wrong. In most tellings its keeper has not been born yet. At every annual renewal, the pilots leave the ninth channel unclaimed. On purpose.

**Frictions.** With the Renlei, reciprocally: a Renlei captain asking for a navigator's word in writing is asking the navigator to say it twice, the second time weaker. The request is always granted and always remembered. With the Umutu, reciprocally: burn notice is posted with the Concord, the Umutu herders do not attend the fire sessions, and then protest the smoke — which Tangata read as choosing grievance over attendance, a choice they respect less than either open objection or silence. With the Crown: the Namarite Tithe's shrines in Tangata territory operate under treaty, and Tangata treat Tithe collectors as travelers on a named way — they owe the way-toll like anyone. The Tithe pays it and books it as alms. Both sides are content with mutually exclusive descriptions of the same coin, which may be the most Terhia sentence in this document.

## The Common Tongue

Every culture speaks it; none speaks it first. Most cross-cultural disputes in the record follow one pattern: a speaker deploys a concept their language holds in one word, the Common Tongue paraphrase comes back subtly wrong, and both parties proceed on different sentences while believing they heard the same one.

# Part VII — Gold Crown Theology [C]

Ratified 11 September 2026 (R.0 #5), including the five [retro] groundings.

Scope: the four Gold Crown homelands only — Isavík, Teocalli, Bahr-Al-Asra, Oasira. Blue Accord and Green Chimera homelands have no theology defined here; the same four cultures' Accord- and Chimera-side homelands (Grimskov, Zand-Kala, Yaxkulu, Zulwazi) share the pantheons below by culture but not the Crown's ranking of them, which stops at the charter line. Nothing in this Part assigns a god to any character, school, or faction outside the Crown.

Design constraints this layer was built to satisfy, all already in canon: the Crown is Devout and governed by Namaris; its classes are Apostle (Divine), Archpriest (Elemental, the shared moon), Paladin (Hammer); the Divine Guru ability is lifespan transference and the Divine cost is lifespan compression; the Indigo Assay is a Rationalist faction under Crown charter that authenticates miracles and voids benefice for fraud; the Crown purged the Ethosless (1164–66) and proscribed reverie as "the heresy nearest its own practice" (1189); the Crown holds boundary testimony to be heresy; culture is not institution.

## The Doctrine of Lent Years

The Crown's creed, stated as the Chancel teaches it.

1. **Every life is a span of years lent, not owned.** The lender is one god. The gold moon is the god's visible sign; the Namaris phase is the audit, when the loan is counted.
2. **Grace is countable.** A Divine practitioner does not create life. An Apostle reallocates lent years from one account to another. The healing is the transfer; the Apostle's own aging is the debit. Lifespan transference at Guru rank is the creed performed in full, which is why the Crown treats the Divine cost progression as sacrament rather than injury.
3. **A miracle is a transfer that can be certified.** This is the Indigo Assay's theological warrant. The Crown chartered a Rationalist examining body because a ledger requires auditors, and a transfer that cannot be verified is either a forgery or the work of a debtor (clause 5). Fraud voids benefice because a false miracle is a forged entry against the god.
4. **The god has one name in doctrine and four in the homelands.** The Doctrine of the Four Names holds that the Namaris-faces of Isavík, Teocalli, Bahr-Al-Asra, and Oasira are one god under local names. The Chancel's liturgical name, in the Asran register of the pontifical seat, is **Al-Muqrid** — the Lender. The Common Tongue uses the title, not the name.
5. **Three transgressions.** *Taking without the Lender:* to speak across the death boundary is to take a span back from the Debtor's side — Necrotic practice, anathema since the Purge. *Giving outside the ledger:* to restore life by unledgered means — Nature practice, and reverie generally, proscribed since the Edict of Amilpan. *Counterfeiting a transfer:* fraud, which voids benefice and unmakes the claimant (the Disputed Seat, 1236–39).
6. **The lesser faces are the Lender's household.** Each homeland's gods of the other four moons are classed by doctrine, not denied. Saelura-faces are *Stewards* (sanctioned; the Archpriests' patrons). Orrivane-faces are *Witnesses* (tolerated; the Assay's patrons). Threnis-faces are *Sowers* (proscribed). Velquor-faces are *Debtors* (anathema).

Three consequences the doctrine produces without anyone intending them: the Crown's most policed heresy is the school whose healing looks most like its own; the Crown's own auditors can and did bankrupt it; and the observance of the Debtor's night, 1 Velquor, is kept by a Devout faction chartered by the Accord, not the Crown (the Firstday Watch — Canon.md faction table).

### Creature Generation

All creatures derive from a compositional formula:

```
Creature = Base Animal × Biome × Moon × Ethos
```

Not metaphorical. Biome, lunar phase, and prevailing ethos literally shape what emerges. Creatures are environmentally produced, not designed.

## The Ledger Peace, 1145–1163

The Concordat's most durable product was not a border. It was a document regime.

In 1149 the Cobalt Ledger completed the General Register: the first full census of licensed practitioners across all Accord territory, every entry recording school, rank, and measured casting cost. Crown territory adopted a parallel registry within a decade, administered through the Indigo Assay. Chimera territory refused, and refuses. The Register converted magic from a talent into an accountable asset, and the institutions spent the next hundred years arguing over the accounting.

Trade grew on two competing systems. The Echo Portal network — Accord-chartered, Arcane-staffed, priced by the Portal Compact of 1157 — moved passengers and light cargo between homeland seats. Airship guilds, unchartered and undercutting portal tolls, moved bulk freight. The Accord's Manifest Rule (1159), requiring registered cargo declarations on all portal traffic, was written to tax the portals and instead pushed the profitable contraband onto the airships and the overland Threnis-territory routes. The smuggling economy that would consolidate as the Black Meridian was priced into existence by a customs regulation.

The period's largest recorded beast event was modest by later standards: a Master-tier burrow-dominant emerged in the Shalei Desert during the Threnis phase of 1155, closed the Shakou caravan road for a season, and was displaced the following Saelura phase when hunters collapsed its oasis dependency — a standard environmental reversal, executed by contract, invoiced to three merchant houses.

## The Purge Years, 1163–1169

On 41 Namaris 1163, Sacrist-General Harun al-Rimal was killed at Tepemitla, struck down on the temple causeway during the Namaris-phase rites, in front of roughly four thousand witnesses who saw nothing. The blade was never recovered.

What happened in the following hour is the most litigated event of the century. A Necrotic Master attending the rites unlicensed performed a boundary reading at the scene. A Necrotic Guru — name struck from every registry, identity still disputed — held the crossing open long enough for the dying Sacrist-General to speak. Chancel witnesses recorded the words. The words named an under-treasurer of the Gilt Chancel.

The Crown's public position, held to this day: testimony obtained across the death boundary is heresy compounding heresy, inadmissible in any court, sacred or civil. Basira al-Rimal stood on the causeway that day; the Chancel's own minutes place her among those who heard the naming. What the testimony threatened needs no speculation: in the Crown's holiest precinct, during its own rites, an unlicensed practitioner of the black moon had produced the only account of the killing that anyone believed. The Crown's private position can be inferred from what followed. The under-treasurer was dead within the year, officially of fever. And beginning in 1164, under Sacrist-General Basira al-Rimal, Crown territory conducted the systematic expulsion of every Ethosless practitioner within its borders — the Purge of the Gold Crown, 1164–1166. Necrotic practitioners, licensed or not, along with Hexblades and Warlocks of no institutional charter, were stripped of residence, property, and in documented cases, life. Crown records list 11,000 expulsions. Sedge Circle oral record, which received many of the refugees, puts the figure at three times that.

The Purge's consequences outlived its targets. The refugee flow moved south and east through tribal territory, and by 1167 the smuggling networks it traveled had consolidated under a single operating discipline: movement timed to Velquor's phase, routes through Threnis territory that the tribes taxed but did not report. The Black Meridian dates its coherence as a faction to these years. So does the Off-Ledger's transformation from a scattering of unlicensed practitioners into a deliberate inversion of the registry system. The Crown, purging its own shadow, gave the shadow structure.

## The Long Frost, 1169–1176

Guru Aslaug of Stenhavn had kept the harbor ice-free for thirty-one years. The Ledger's cost file on her is a complete record of the Elemental progression: fatigue notations from the 1140s, muscle-failure accommodations from the 1150s, organ-strain waivers signed annually from 1161, each one a decision to continue. She collapsed at the seawall on 63 Velquor 1169 and the harbor froze within the phase.

Stenhavn was Grimskov's grain port. Five winters, 1169–1174, the ice held and the imports failed, and the Kaldmark garrison spent more of those winters managing bread riots than the frontier. Grimskov pressed claims on Svarthavet fishing waters that Isavík considered its own; two fleet actions in 1172 killed several dozen sailors and settled nothing; only the Steel Quorum, selling identical blockade doctrine to both sides, profited. The Stenhavn tide log for those winters survives in the Ledger's provincial files. The entry for the morning after Aslaug died reads, in full: *no reading taken*. For thirty-one years the line had been a depth and a temperature; for the next five it is one word, *ice*, four hundred times a year, in the harbor-master's hand. The wider lesson was the one every chancery drew at once: a single Elemental Guru had been load-bearing infrastructure for an entire homeland's food supply, and no one had priced the risk.

The Guru Escrow Convention of 1174 was the repricing. Harbor-grade Elemental Gurus — there were four alive at signing — were registered as shared infrastructure, leased through the Accord under terms all three institutions countersigned, their deployment a matter of treaty rather than ownership. It remains the only document the Crown, the Accord, and a quorum of Chimera claimants have all signed. Stenhavn reopened under a leased Guru in 1175. The grain price took another year to follow.

## The Antler War, 1178–1189

The Crown came out of the Frost years financially strained and doctrinally hard, and under Basira al-Rimal's late seat it pushed the Namaris rites outward — new shrines in the borderlands where Oasira's grasslands run against tribal Threnis territory. The tribes read shrine construction as territorial claim, because it was. Shrines burned in 1178 and 1179. Crown reprisal columns crossed into unceded land in 1180. By Saelura 1181 it was a war.

It was a war neither side could win on the ground it was fought on. Crown paladin columns held anything flat and lost everything wooded; Reverie rangers and berserker bands made the Mwamba foothills impassable and could not take a walled town. Ash-and-Antler, offered Crown contracts at triple rate, refused them and spent the war warding the Accord's frontier instead — a refusal the compact still cites in its charter renewals. The Unsworn took contracts from both belligerents, which produced the war's strangest hour: at Marendi in 1184, two Unsworn companies found themselves fielded on opposite sides of the same engagement, and per compact rules, stood down in view of both armies. The battle dissolved around them. The Marendi Standstill has been cited in every mercenary contract negotiation since.

The war was ended by neither army. In the Threnis phase of 1186, with node activity along the Mwamba Range at the highest reading then on record, the forested foothills produced an apex emergence — a Master-tier predator that broke a Crown column and a tribal muster in the same eight days and held the ground between them. Both commands withdrew. Both chanceries discovered, reading the same Quorum assessment, that they had been paying for a front line a single animal could hold better than either of them.

The Treaty of Tinwara (1187) followed within the year. Its terms built two factions that persist: the Namarite Tithe, by which the Crown maintains its shrines in Chimera territory under treaty and collects along the Namaris circuit rather than holding ground; and the Bramble Concord, chartered as the standing negotiation between tribal claimants that the war had improvised. Ash-and-Antler led the cull of the Mwamba apex in 1188, executing the environmental reversal across a full phase turn. The Crown, for its part, drew an internal lesson from a war begun over rites: the Edict of Amilpan (1189) proscribed reverie practice throughout Crown territory outright — the heresy nearest its own practice, and therefore, in the Edict's own language, the least tolerable.

## The Yunmen Lag and the Manifest War, 1196–1205

The portal network's traffic peaked every Orrivane phase, when Arcane casting cost ran lowest and the fare schedules followed. On 17 Orrivane 1198, the station at Yunmen ran a dilation fault. The presiding Arcane Guru, working from inside the bounded volume, desynchronized: travelers stepped out of the Yunmen gate as much as nine days out of true, the Guru was retired with an identity-time fracture the Ledger's own examiners could not fully map, and two attending Masters developed memory dissonance within the year. First Examiner Feng Zhao, who had personally certified the Yunmen station, resigned before the audit finished.

The Accord suspended the entire network — the Interdiction, 1198–1201 — and reopened it under the exterior-placement doctrine that has governed Guru-rank casting since: the caster stands outside the edited volume, without exception, whatever the cost in precision. The doctrine is written in the dry procedural language of a station manual. Every line of it was paid for at Yunmen.

Three years of dead portals made the airship guilds rich and the caravan towns — Shakou, Yunmen itself, the Bahr Sufra crossings — richer. When the network reopened under new tariffs, the guilds declined to surrender the traffic, and the dispute ran hot from 1203 to 1205: embargoes, seized cargoes, manifests forged and counter-forged, and one true shooting incident at Shakoui harbor in 1204, when Accord customs cutters fired on a guild freighter and killed nine crew. The Ledger Accords of 1205 settled it as an accounting matter, which it always was — a unified manifest across portal and airship traffic, with tolls pegged to the moon phase, tracking the casting cost of the infrastructure itself. Freight in Terhia has been priced by the sky ever since.

## The Baishui Poisoning and the Second Register, 1211–1214

First Examiner Wen Suyin died at her own examination table at Baishui on 12 Orrivane 1211, mid-certification, of a Warlock-grade contact poison worked into the document wax. The Ledger's investigation remains, by its own classification, open.

It is open because of a rule the Ledger will not bend. A Necrotic boundary reading of the death — performed competently, offered formally, corroborated in its incidental details — was refused as evidence on the standing doctrine that what cannot be measured is anecdotal. The institution that determines truth declined the only testimony available, and has spent thirty-five years unable to name a killer while insisting the method that might name one does not meet standard. The Off-Ledger was blamed by proclamation. No individual was ever charged.

What the Accord could not solve it regulated. The Second Register (1212–1214) forced universal re-licensure: fresh cost audits at every rank, alchemy-handling certifications, poison-precursor controls, and enforcement powers for registry officials that the original General Register never contemplated. For most practitioners, the fresh audit was the first formal measurement of what their work had already taken — the tremor, the scar tissue, the early aging read aloud across an examination table by a stranger with a form. The files from those two years preserve the same notation, page after page, in the space reserved for the examinee's remarks: *declined to review results*. The compliance regime the present generation of practitioners works under — and the unlicensed evade — is the Baishui poisoning's longest consequence.

## The Node Decade, 1218–1232

From roughly 1218, the substrate itself became the story. Firstday Watch phase ledgers — kept unbroken since before the Concordat — begin recording anomalies in that year and never stop: tidal distortion in Svarthavet exceeding Velquor norms, the Tienhu fog carrying light it should not carry, Saelura-phase healing rates in the Awanui rainforest running past anything in the record. Node activity was rising across the map, and the ecology answered.

Off Puketai in the Threnis phase of 1224, the reef came coordinated. Not a beast — a biome effect at full expression: reef intelligence across a harbor's whole approach, channels closing against unwelcome hulls. Tangata navigators, working with Sedge Circle initiates, negotiated the Puketai Passage Agreements — standing terms of transit observed to this day, renewed each Threnis phase. The Accord's formal position is that one cannot hold treaty with a coral formation, and its merchant captains observe the Agreements to the letter.

Then Frostvatn. On 1 Velquor 1230 — the longest night of the year, at the strongest hour of the black moon's phase — a Guru-tier manifestation rose from Lake Frostvatn under the Hrafnrygg range: lunar embodiment, Velquor's own signature, tethered to the lake basin. Nothing in living memory matched it and the old records that did were not reassuring. It could not be killed; Guru-tier vulnerability is geographic, not biological. Isavík's northern valleys emptied. Hvítadal stood abandoned for two winters.

The response was the largest joint operation of the century, and the first since the Escrow Convention to put all three institutions in one command tent: Steel Quorum planning purchased jointly, Crown Divine support out of Isavík, Accord Arcane doctrine, Chimera wardens and Ash-and-Antler compacts holding the ground approaches, Unsworn companies on escort under a single consolidated contract — the compact's first and only. The work itself was engineering: two years diverting the Iselva headwaters and unseating the basin's substrate anchor, displacing the tether rather than fighting the beast. The manifestation collapsed with its domain in Velquor 1232. The operation's cost ledger is exact and canonical in the registries: one Elemental Guru dead of collapse at the diversion works, one Arcane Guru retired with identity-time fracture, one Divine Guru who transferred what the Assay certified as thirty-one years of her own span into the frostbitten survivors of the first winter's evacuation, and who died in 1238 at the age of fifty-four, of old age.

## The Benefice Crisis and the Disputed Seat, 1226–1239

The Crown's troubles in the same decade were fiscal before they were sacred. In 1226 the Indigo Assay, executing its charter, voided a cluster of miracle claims in Oasira — healings attributed to Divine attunement that examination showed to be staged, well-intentioned, or simply false. Fraud voids benefice: the Gilt Chancel had issued paper against those miracles, pilgrimage revenue on the Tinwara circuit collapsed, and the Chancel spent 1227 and 1228 selling land title to Accord creditors to stay solvent. The Crown kept faith with its own auditors at the price of its own treasury, which is either the most principled or the most expensive decision in its modern history, depending on which chancery is asked.

The bill came due at succession. Sacrist-General Sunniva al-Rimal died in 1236 after a thirty-two-year seat, and the leading candidate's claim rested on an authenticated healing miracle from the Frostvatn years. ## The Present, 1240–1245

The century closes on a world more documented, more audited, and less settled than the one it opened on.

The Second Register regime is at its strictest enforcement in thirty years, and the Off-Ledger and the Black Meridian are, by the Ledger's own confidential estimates, at their largest. The airship guilds are petitioning for full Accord charter, which would end a hundred years of profitable irregularity, and the caravan towns are lobbying against them. The Bramble Concord's Threnis-territory dockets grow longer each cycle as node-driven range shifts move beasts, and therefore hunting claims, across every negotiated line. Firstday Watch readings have not returned to pre-1218 baseline; the standing assessment is that Frostvatn was the decade's largest expression, not its conclusion.

And one rumor persists, in the way that only officially impossible things persist. The Purge of 1164–1166 is complete in every Crown record — the Ethosless expelled, the cell structure broken, the matter closed. Yet three times in the past decade, Chancel documents have surfaced through Black Meridian channels that no expelled practitioner should have been able to reach. The Crown's position is that the Purge left nothing behind. The Crown's position on the Tepemitla testimony was also, once, that the dead do not speak.

As of 1 Velquor 1245, the three charters stand where the Concordat set them: none dominant, none failed, all three solvent by narrower margins than they publish. The substrate is more active than at any point in the calendar of record. The registries have never been fuller. The unregistered have never been more numerous.

# Part X — Visual Grammar (Graphic Novel Adaptation) [C]

From Canon-visual-grammar.md, unchanged.

## Purpose

Visual language specification for graphic novel adaptation. Panel layout, density, and composition encode the same escalation mechanics that prose encodes through sentence length and scene compression.

---

## Panel Compression Scale

Panel density is a direct analog of narrative tension.

| Narrative Stage | Layout Density | Visual Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Stable | 6-panel grid | Standard pacing. Room to breathe. Environmental detail visible |
| Rising | Narrow gutters | Panels press together. White space decreases. Reader acceleration begins |
| Crisis | Tight compression | Panels overlap or crowd. Gutters nearly gone. Claustrophobic density |
| Guru Event | Full bleed (max 2 per volume) | Panel boundary eliminated. Image fills the page. Reserved for irreversible moments only |

**Constraint:** Full bleed pages — maximum two per volume. Overuse collapses the hierarchy. The restriction ensures that when a boundary breaks visually, the reader feels it.

---

## Correspondence to Narrative Architecture

| Narrative Element | Visual Analog |
|---|---|
| Act I (space to observe) | 6-panel standard. Wide establishing shots |
| Act II (interference) | Narrow gutters. Panels begin to intrude on each other |
| Act III (no space) | Tight compression. Pressed to panel edges |
| Uncontrollable event | Panel disruption — broken border, bleed, or layout violation |
| Institutional failure | Rigid grid imposed on chaotic content (form contradicts content) |
| Crossing sequence | Full bleed |
| Aftermath | Return to 6-panel, but with visible damage — cracked gutters, imperfect alignment |

---

## POV Encoding

Each POV carries a distinct visual signature beyond character design:

| Persona Attribute | Visual Expression |
|---|---|
| Epistemic lens | What the panels *show* (Arcane POV: overlay geometry. Nature POV: body detail) |
| Abstraction level | Panel framing distance (concrete = close-up; abstract = wide/diagrammatic) |
| Stress response | Panel rhythm changes (shortened sentences = rapid small panels; silence = empty panel or gutter expansion) |
| Metaphor domain | Background detail and environmental texture |

---

## School-Specific Visual Language

| School | Visual Signature | Color Profile |
|---|---|---|
| Arcane | Geometric overlays, mathematical notation visible in environment | Cool blues, silver, white |
| Elemental | Environmental distortion, heat shimmer, particle effects | Warm spectrum — flame, stone, water, wind coded |
| Divine | Clean light, symmetrical composition, wards as visible geometry | Gold, warm white |
| Necrotic | Boundary edges visible as physical lines, death as texture | Grey, muted, edges sharp |
| Nature | Organic detail, body interior visible through touch, channels as flowing paths | Green-gold, warm earth |

---

## Adaptation Rules

1. Panel compression must track narrative escalation. A visually relaxed crisis page is a structural error.
2. Full bleed is irreversible. Once used, the narrative has crossed a threshold. The visual cannot retreat to stable grid without marking the cost.
3. The least abstract perceiver gets the visual climax. Final crossing sequence rendered through the most concrete visual language available.
4. Institutional documents appear as text panels — rendered in institutional typography, not hand-lettered. The visual distinction between lived experience (drawn) and institutional record (typeset) must be maintained.
5. No single panel contains the whole truth. Perceptual fragmentation applies to visual storytelling. Different POVs seeing the same event produce visually distinct panels.
